## D1 — Model-based simulation (no hardware, no external dataset)
**Decision:** Pure MATLAB/Simulink simulation; the simulator self-generates the labeled dataset.
**Alternatives:** SDR hardware testbed; use of a public RF dataset.
**Rationale:** Scope and risk control — hardware and field data collection are out of a one-semester capstone's reach. Framing the project as proof-of-concept with theoretical validation (BER vs Eb/N0) is the accepted de-risking path; hardware validation is named as future work.
**Sources:** Proposal risk section (#4); standard practice in UAV jamming-detection work that generates data in MATLAB/Simulink.

## D2 — MATLAB + Simulink (not Python)
**Decision:** Stay all-in on MATLAB/Simulink for the full pipeline.
**Alternatives:** Python (TensorFlow/PyTorch) with a rebuilt or bridged RF layer. Supervisor explicitly allowed switching.
**Rationale:** RF/comms modeling is a core deliverable and Communications Toolbox has no Python equivalent; RL Toolbox provides DQN with replay/target-networks out of the box (de-risks the agent); the closed loop needs comms + AI in one runtime. Python would only win if the project were AI-heavy/comms-light — it is not.

## D3 — Platform scope: small UAV, single RF link, 2.4 GHz ISM, Rician LoS (short-range)
**Decision:** Small tactical UAV over a single short-range LoS link at 2.4 GHz ISM.
**Alternatives:** Large MALE UAV (Hermes/Heron class) with SATCOM/dedicated bands.
**Rationale:** 2.4 GHz ISM + short-range LoS is physically the domain of small/commercial UAVs; MALE platforms use SATCOM and dedicated military bands. The project's "wow" comes from implementation quality (real-time closed loop, dashboard, DQN vs rule-based, regime map), not platform size. Smaller scope = tighter focus, lower risk.

## D4 — UAV velocity 18–22 m/s (nominal 20) → Doppler 160 Hz
**Decision:** Operational envelope 18–22 m/s (65–79 km/h), nominal 20 m/s. Max Doppler fd = v·fc/c = 160 Hz (normalized 1.6e-4).
**Alternatives:** Hermes/Heron cruise (~30–36 m/s) — rejected as belonging to the MALE class, inconsistent with D3.
**Rationale:** 18–22 m/s sits in the DoD Group 1 SUAS envelope (Skylark/Raven class: ~32–81 km/h). The cited anti-jamming papers (Liu, Yuan) are platform-agnostic and specify no velocity, so the value is anchored to real small-ISR platform specs, not to a paper. At 1 Msym/s the normalized Doppler stays ~1.6e-4 → deep slow/quasi-static fading across the whole envelope, so the exact value is a documentation choice, not one that changes channel behavior.
**Sources:** Elbit Skylark I and RQ-11 Raven published specifications (Group 1 SUAS).
**Amended by D25 (2026-09-21):** the operating envelope is widened to a continuous 50–120 km/h (13.9–33.3 m/s; Doppler 111–267 Hz). 72 km/h / 160 Hz stays the nominal condition.

## D5 — Modulation QPSK, symbol rate 1 Msym/s, Rician K = 10 dB
**Decision:** QPSK, 1 Msym/s, Rician K-factor 10 dB (strong LoS).
**Rationale:** QPSK is the standard baseline for a control link (good spectral efficiency, robust). K = 10 dB reflects a dominant line-of-sight component typical of a short-range UAV-GCS link. 1 Msym/s gives a realistic control-link rate and places the channel firmly in slow-fading relative to the Doppler.

## D6 — Separate Rician and AWGN blocks (not a combined channel)
**Decision:** Model multipath fading (Rician) and receiver noise (AWGN) as two distinct blocks.
**Rationale:** Keeps clean, physically-meaningful injection points for threats: fading is a channel property, noise is a receiver property, and threats are external. Separation lets each threat attach at the correct point without entangling the channel model.

## D7 — No carrier synchronizer (kept out of scope)
**Decision:** Do not implement a carrier synchronizer; keep the Rician+Doppler baseline as-is (1.43× BER degradation vs theory).
**Alternatives:** comm.CarrierSynchronizer to recover the Doppler-induced phase rotation.
**Rationale:** The 160 Hz Doppler causes a measured 1.43× BER degradation — this is a useful, documented result that motivates adaptive recovery, not a defect to fix. The project's focus is AI-based detection + adaptive decision, not PHY-layer synchronization. A synchronizer added cost and QPSK phase-ambiguity issues with no benefit to the core deliverables.

## D8 — Threat injection: signal-level additive (Approach A)
**Decision:** Inject threats as real interference waveforms added to the complex IQ signal, at the point matching each threat's physical origin.
**Alternatives:** Approach B — metric-level injection (degrade SNR / add packet-loss on the features directly, no waveform).
**Rationale:** Signal-level injection preserves BOTH scalar metrics (BER, RSSI, SNR, PLR) AND spectral features (spectrogram/PSD). Approach B is simpler but forecloses the spectrogram path the detection layer may need. This matches mainstream UAV jamming-detection practice, including work that generates the dataset in MATLAB/Simulink and feeds spectrograms to a CNN.
**Sources:** 5G jamming detection via receiver-front-end waveform injection; UAV jamming detection with MATLAB/Simulink spectrogram dataset (legitimate/compromised/noise classes); manned-unmanned interference detection defining barrage/tone/pulse jamming for a CNN; RFI datasets built by combining signal-of-interest with jammers across SNRs.

## D9 — Six base threats; eavesdropping and MITM out of scope
**Decision:** Base threats — Jamming (barrage), Spoofing, Noise Burst, Antenna Fault, Path Loss, + Reactive Jamming. Skip eavesdropping and MITM.
**Rationale:** All six are observable in link metrics. Antenna Fault and Path Loss are non-hostile faults, kept deliberately so the system learns to separate hostile interference from physical failure. Reactive Jamming is added because it maps directly to the cited smart/mobile-jammer literature (Liu, Yuan) and justifies the DQN. Eavesdropping is passive (does not change BER/RSSI/SNR — invisible to link-metric detection); MITM needs protocol-layer modeling — both are out of the link-level scope. Multi-threat (combined attacks) is reserved as a later research-contribution extension, not a base capability.
**Sources:** Communication-layer UAV threat taxonomies (jamming, replay, eavesdropping, MITM); reactive/deceptive jamming definitions; spoofing detection via Rician-K and path-loss features (confirms our channel features already support spoofing detection).

## D10 — Threat build order: barrage jamming first
**Decision:** Implement and validate barrage jamming before any other threat.
**Rationale:** It is both the simplest to implement (additive wideband noise — the full Approach-A mechanism in its cleanest form) and the easiest to detect (raises the noise floor across the whole spectrum; BER jumps, SNR drops — the class furthest from "normal"). Validating the injection mechanism on the simplest threat means every later threat is a variation on a proven base.

## D11 — System Objects engine (replaced commfilt2 blocks)
**Decision:** Replaced Simulink `commfilt2` RRC blocks with System Objects
(`comm.RaisedCosineTransmitFilter`/`ReceiveFilter` + `pskmod`/`pskdemod`)
wrapped in MATLAB Function blocks (same pattern as Rician injection).
**Reason:** `commfilt2` blocks injected hidden algorithmic frame-buffer delay
(measured 30-bit delay vs 20-bit theoretical) and a decimation-timing ISI floor
that locked BER at ~0.17 regardless of SNR. Verified via Python + MATLAB System
Objects: identical RRC math gives BER exactly on `berawgn` theory (delay=20 bits).
**Architecture:** Split Digital Twin — [Tx: mod+RRC] -> [channels: Rician, AWGN
graphical] -> [Rx: RRC+demod]. Preserves visual Simulink twin for the report
while giving deterministic mathematical control under the hood.
**Validated:** AWGN sweep 0-10 dB, all points on theory curve (delay=20 stable).

## D12 — Spoofing detection: root-caused and fixed at threat-injection level
**Decision:** Rewrote spoofing injection in `build_threat_model.m` to generate a real bit stream, QPSK-modulate it, and RRC-shape it — identical processing to the legitimate Tx path (a dedicated persistent filter object, separate from the Tx's, preserves filter state across calls).
**Problem it replaces:** spoofing had been injected as i.i.d. per-sample random noise (`randn(size(u))>0` applied independently to every sample), not a coherent modulated carrier. This made it spectrally indistinguishable from jamming/reactive_jamming/benign_interference — all four were effectively variants of "additive noise at different power levels." This was the true cause of spoofing's historical recall instability (53-79.5% across different runs/seeds), previously misdiagnosed as a detector-architecture or feature-space limitation.
**Alternatives considered:** CNN-LSTM detector (see D13) — built and tested as a fix for the *symptom* before the root cause was found.
**Result:** spoofing recall 53-79% → 98.7% (run 1) / 99.7% (run 2, independent retrain, different seed). Overall CNN accuracy 91.81% → 98.05%.
**Consequence:** invalidates the premise of D13 (CNN-LSTM) as a spoofing fix — the gap it was built to close no longer exists.

## D13 — CNN-LSTM comparison architecture: built, evaluated, not pursued further
**Decision:** Production detector remains CNN+scalar hybrid. CNN-LSTM was implemented, trained, and evaluated as a candidate fix for spoofing's (then-)weak recall, per proposal risk #5's named mitigation ("compare multiple model architectures").
**What was tested:** dataset expanded to 27,246 frames (`frames_per_config` 50→100) specifically to support sequence-based training; sequence windowing (`build_sequence_index.m`, `extract_spectrograms_seq.m`) with data-leakage and per-class split-imbalance fixes applied across two runs.
**Result:** CNN-LSTM underperformed CNN+scalar on spoofing by -9.8pp and -21.8pp across the two configurations tested. Once D12 (above) fixed spoofing at its true root cause, CNN alone exceeded LSTM's best result by a wide margin with far fewer parameters, making the comparison moot for production use.
**Kept in repo, not deleted:** the LSTM scripts (`*_lstm.m`, `*_seq.m`) are the actual evidence that the risk-#5 mitigation ("compare architectures") was performed, not merely asserted. `main.m`'s Phase B-exp flags remain, all defaulted false, with an explicit "NOT PURSUED FURTHER" banner.

## D14 — DQN state encoding: ordinal → one-hot
**Decision:** Replace the scalar ordinal `threat_encode` (single integer 0-8 fed directly to the network) with a 9-dim one-hot vector, appended to 4 continuous link metrics for a 13-dim state. Implemented as a single shared function, `build_dqn_state.m`, called identically by `train_dqn.m` and both closed-loop scripts.
**Problem it replaces:** a neural network reading a scalar ordinal code interprets it as continuous, causing Q-value "bleed" between adjacent, semantically-unrelated threat codes. A prior patch (v4, 2026-09-14) worked around one specific instance (antenna_fault bleeding from the adjacent benign_interference penalty code) by manually reassigning code values — a symptom fix, not a structural one, and it would need re-verification every time a new threat class or code value was added.
**Rationale for the structural fix:** one-hot removes the adjacency-bleed failure mode by construction, for all classes simultaneously, rather than by careful manual placement of a small number of integers. An unrecognized threat name yields an all-zero one-hot (not an error) — a valid "unknown threat" state where the agent must rely on the continuous link metrics alone, which is also a direct mitigation for proposal risk #13 ("system should react to functional link changes, not just the threat's name").
**Validation:** post-training Gate A/B in `train_dqn.m` (see D9's reward-shaping mechanism) — refuses to save an agent that fails either check, using each threat's real measured baseline state (not synthetic).

## D15 — KPI #3 redefinition: recovery cycles → decision latency
**Decision:** Redefine the proposal's "DQN vs rule-based recovery time" KPI from a cycles-to-convergence metric to a decision-latency metric.
**Problem it replaces:** the original `measure_kpi3_recovery_time.m` simulated a halving-based "convergence" loop that never actually called `sim()` or consulted either policy's real action choice — it produced identical results for both policies regardless of which was nominally being measured.
**Root conceptual issue:** in this system, both the DQN and the rule-based policy are single-shot, deterministic dB reductions — there is no multi-cycle dynamic process to converge. A "recovery cycles" metric does not correspond to anything the system actually does.
**Rationale:** decision latency is the metric that both matches what the system actually computes (a one-shot decision, each policy takes some wall-clock time to produce it) and is directly relevant to real-time deployability. Measured via 1000 direct calls to `rule_based_policy.m` (lookup-table speed) vs. the real neural-network inference latency recorded in `closed_loop_diagnostic_report.txt`.
**Caveat for the report:** the resulting "DQN vs rule agreement" comparison (used elsewhere as a KPI) compares only the chosen action *name* between policies — `rule_based_policy.m`'s own per-threat magnitudes are computed but never applied to any BER calculation; both policies' physical mitigation effect goes through the same `action_mitigation_db`. State this precisely — it is a decision-policy comparison, not two independently-realized countermeasure systems.

## D16 — Closed-loop sliding-window feature fix
**Decision:** `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m` now builds the 3 temporal features (var_rssi_10, dber_dt, burst_ratio) from a real sliding window over each Simulink run's ~20 returned frames, using the same logic as `extract_spectrograms.m` (training time), instead of feeding the CNN neutral placeholder values.
**Problem it replaces:** a single-shot closed-loop run has no history, so the placeholders (0,0,1) were used as a stand-in — but the CNN was trained on real temporal features, and reactive_jamming's entire distinguishing signature is a temporal pattern (a jammer that responds with delay), not a static spectrogram shape. Closed-loop detection accuracy under the placeholder scheme was 79.6% (vs. 98.05% on the offline test set), with two failure modes at 100% across all SNR points (reactive_jamming→jamming, none→path_loss).
**Alternatives tried and rejected:** a first fix attempt built the features from all 20 available frames but without NaN-guarding — `run_dataset_sweep.m` marks the last frame's BER as NaN in every run (a delay-bits truncation artifact), and the unguarded NaN silently propagated through the network, dropping accuracy further to 66.7% with two classes at 0%.
**Final implementation:** last-valid-frame selection (`find(~isnan(ber_f),1,'last')`), NaN guards mirroring `prepare_data.m` exactly (`feats(isnan(feats))=0`), and before/after BER both averaged over all valid frames rather than compared single-frame-to-single-frame.
**Result:** closed-loop detection accuracy 79.6% → **100% (54/54)**, all 9 classes × 6 SNR points. FAR returned to its expected near-zero value (from a spurious 64.5% under the broken pipeline).

## D17 — Survivability boundary: two-map methodology (Neutralization vs. Survivability)
**Decision:** `map_survivability_boundary.m` produces two separate boundary maps rather than one: Map A restricts to mechanisms that genuinely remove the threat (`atten_reduction`, `field_reduction`); Map B includes every available mechanism, including `awgn_margin_boost`.
**Problem it replaces:** the original single-map version took the best BER across all mechanisms without distinguishing between them. `awgn_margin_boost` raises effective SNR rather than weakening the attack, so it can make the link "recover" to better-than-clean-channel performance without the threat being neutralized at all — the single map silently let this dominate, producing physically-impossible "of ceiling" figures (up to 283.8%) for the threats where it won most often (sweeping_jammer, benign_interference).
**Rationale:** proposal deliverable #7 asks for the regime where "the attack is recoverable vs. non-recoverable" — that is a statement about the attack's own persistence, which Map A answers literally. Map B is retained as the system's overall achievable ceiling (useful context: what the link can do by any means), and the divergence between the two maps *is itself* the concrete instance of the proposal's goodput/margin tradeoff discussion.
**Result:** Map A 84.6% recoverable / Map B 87.9% recoverable (a 3.3pp gap, concentrated almost entirely in path_loss and the two threats margin-boost favored most). 1 explicit gap cell identified (path_loss, level=8, SNR=10dB) where the link survives via Map B but the threat is not neutralized per Map A.
**Known limitation, documented not fixed:** path_loss at its lowest tested severity (level=4) has zero legitimate Map A data points, because the global `field_reduction` magnitude sweep (5/10/15/20/25dB) starts above that severity level — every candidate would imply unphysical signal amplification and is correctly excluded by the existing legitimacy filter. This is a coverage gap in the exploration sweep's design, not a bug in the mapping logic.

## D18 — Rx_IQ tap point fixed: post-AWGN, not pre-AWGN (major, full re-run required)
**Decision:** `build_threat_model.m`'s `Rx_IQ` workspace probe is now wired from `AWGN/1` (post-noise), matching `build_link_model.m` (A1) and `build_rician_model.m` (A3).
**Problem it replaces:** `build_threat_model.m` (A4 — the only model actually used for the dataset, CNN training, DQN, EXP, and FAR) had `Rx_IQ` wired from `Threat/1` (pre-AWGN). A1 and A3 both correctly tap post-AWGN; only A4 diverged. Discovered during a full line-by-line code review (2026-09-19), not by any test failing — the pipeline ran and reported numbers throughout, but those numbers didn't mean what they appeared to.
**Consequence:** every spectrogram used to train the CNN, and every RSSI value in the system (CNN scalar branch, DQN state, reward table, closed-loop features, FAR), was computed from a signal that never included the swept AWGN noise. BER was unaffected (computed separately from tx/rx bit streams through the full chain, AWGN included). This explains why per-SNR CNN accuracy looked remarkably flat (97.6%-98.9%) — the visual/spectral input barely changed across the SNR sweep at all; only the ground-truth `snr` scalar feature and the correctly-computed BER/PLR varied.
**Rationale for fixing rather than only documenting:** a detector whose main input channel structurally cannot see the thing (noise) it is nominally being evaluated against is not demonstrating SNR robustness — it is demonstrating that SNR wasn't visually present. Reporting 98.05% "stable across SNR" without this caveat would misrepresent what was measured. Since two of the three model builders already established the correct convention, this is a one-line fix restoring consistency, not a new design choice.
**Consequence for the project:** requires a full re-run of the downstream pipeline (A5→A6→B1→B2→B3→C2→C3, and for full consistency EXP→survivability mapping, though those are BER-based and not structurally affected). Expect per-SNR CNN accuracy to show genuine SNR-dependent degradation post-fix, which is a *more* convincing result for the report than the previous artificially-flat curve, not a weaker one.

## D19 — rule_based_policy.m's per-threat mitigation_db: documented as unused, not removed
**Decision:** keep `rule_based_policy.m`'s per-threat `mitigation_db` return value, but document explicitly (in the function header and in README/PROJECT_LOG) that it is never applied to any BER calculation.
**Problem it clarifies:** both call sites (`run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m`, `measure_kpi3_recovery_time.m`) use only the returned action *name*; the physical mitigation effect for both the DQN and the rule-based policy is computed through the shared `action_mitigation_db` (25/15/25/25 dB per action). Discovered during the 2026-09-19 code review — not a functional bug (nothing crashes or produces wrong numbers), but a naming/expectation mismatch: the per-threat values (15/10/15/8/6/9/12 dB) look like they drive the comparison and don't.
**Rationale for documenting rather than implementing a fully independent rule-based physical system:** doing so would change what "DQN-vs-rule agreement" measures (from a pure decision-policy comparison to a comparison of two differently-tuned physical systems) — a scope decision, not a bug fix. Documented as D19 so the report states precisely what was compared.

## D20 — Shared closed-loop frame extraction; FAR measurement bug fixed
**Decision:** extract the "pull all frames from one sim() call, with per-frame BER/RSSI/PLR" logic into a single shared function `extract_closed_loop_frames.m`, used by both `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m` and `diagnose_far_measurement.m`.
**Problem it replaces:** after the D18 re-run, `diagnose_far_measurement.m` reported none->path_loss confusion at ~72-78% FAR, while `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m` reported none at 100% correct on the same trained model. Two distinct causes were found in the FAR script:
1. It still used the pre-D16 neutral placeholder `[0,0,1]` for the temporal features — the exact bug D16 fixed in the diagnostic, which had never been propagated to this second script (a duplicated frame-extraction copy is how that happened).
2. More seriously, it rebuilt the threat model each trial but never called `set_param([modelName '/AWGN'],'SNR',...)` — so every FAR trial ran at Simulink's default AWGN level, not the intended SNR. A clean channel at the wrong (very low) SNR looks spectrally like weak path_loss, which is exactly the confusion observed.
**Rationale for a shared function rather than a third fix in place:** the sliding-window logic had now been needed in three scripts (diagnostic, with_detector implicitly, FAR); fixing it a third time in a third copy is how the drift keeps happening. One shared source (like `build_dqn_state.m`) makes a fourth recurrence impossible.
**Fix details:** `diagnose_far_measurement.m` rewritten to (a) call `extract_closed_loop_frames.m`, (b) set the AWGN SNR per trial, and (c) sweep SNR = 0/4/10 dB at N=30 each for a proper FAR-vs-SNR characterization rather than a single worst-case point.
**Result:** FAR 0.0% across all 180 trials (95% CI upper bound 3.3% by Rule of Three); none CNN detection accuracy 98.9%, benign_interference 100%. The ~72% figure was entirely a measurement artifact, not a model weakness — confirming the CNN and DQN were correct all along.

## D21 — no_action recovery reported as N/A; GPU warm-up strengthened
**Decision:** in `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m`, when the DQN chooses `no_action`, report recovery as N/A (NaN) rather than computing a BER ratio; and strengthen the GPU warm-up so latency measurements are steady from the first real run.
**Problem it replaces (recovery):** non-hostile classes (none, benign_interference) correctly receive `no_action`, but recovery% was still computed as `(BER_before - BER_after)/BER_before` on a channel that was never modified — i.e. two independent noise draws of the same clean channel. At high SNR (BER ~1e-3), dividing the tiny random difference produced large spurious values (none = -28.9% at 10 dB) that read as a failure but were pure division noise (the sign swung both ways across SNR, the tell of noise rather than a real effect).
**Rationale:** "no action was taken, so there is nothing to recover" is the physically correct statement; N/A communicates it directly and also makes the correct no-action behavior explicit in the report table, instead of hiding it behind a confusing signed percentage. Real threats never choose no_action, so their recovery numbers are unaffected. Manually verified none chose no_action at all 6 SNR points with BER_before ≈ BER_after each time (independent confirmation of the 0% FAR from D20).
**Problem it replaces (latency):** the noise_burst latency "spike" (~11-29 ms vs ~2 ms elsewhere) was not class-specific — it was the single largest value inside the whole first (SNR=0) measurement block, an artifact of cuDNN selecting convolution kernels lazily on the first real-sized input. The single dummy warm-up pass didn't force that autotuning to finish.
**Fix (latency):** warm-up strengthened to 10 iterations with `rand` inputs (not `zeros`, which can take a fast path) plus `wait(gpuDevice)` to block until the kernels finish, before any timing starts. All latencies are now steady (~2.5-3 ms) from the first measured run; noise_burst dropped from ~11 ms to ~3 ms, and the KPI#3 mean latency is a clean 2.67 ms.

## D22 — Decision-latency measurement scope widened to include preprocessing
**Decision:** in `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m`, "CNN latency" now times the full per-decision processing chain — spectrogram (STFT), dB conversion, normalization, image resize, tensor prep — plus the network forward pass, instead of timing only `predict()` in isolation.
**Problem it replaces:** the original `tic` was placed immediately before `predict()`, after all signal preprocessing had already run unmeasured. The comment above that block had always said "TIMED" for the whole block, but the `tic` itself didn't match the comment's intent — the reported number was inference-only, not the true reaction time a deployed system would need end-to-end.
**Rationale:** a latency figure used to argue real-time viability has to cover everything the system actually does per decision, not just the neural-network portion. DQN latency remains inference-only by design — its input (the state vector) is already numeric with no comparable preprocessing cost.
**Result:** latency figures are now higher but honest (measured mean 18.88ms / median 8.42ms in the first run after this fix) — see D23 for why that first measurement also had a large mean-median gap, and its resolution.

## D23 — spectrogram() warm-up added; mean/median latency converged
**Decision:** extend the existing CNN/DQN GPU warm-up in `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m` and `demo_gui.m` to also call `spectrogram()` on dummy IQ data (2064 samples, matching one real frame) several times before timing starts.
**Problem it replaces:** immediately after D22 widened the measurement scope, the first full run showed mean latency (18.88ms) far above the median (8.42ms) — traced to a one-time JIT/internal-cache cost paid by MATLAB's `spectrogram()` on its first call in a session, landing on whichever threat happened to run first and skewing the mean for the whole batch. This was a measurement artifact, not a system characteristic, and the fix belongs in the warm-up procedure (matching how CNN/DQN warm-up was already handled), not as an explanation footnoted in the report.
**Result:** re-run after the fix: mean 6.09ms / median 5.67ms — a 0.42ms gap (was 10.46ms), confirming the skew was fully eliminated. This is also the number now used everywhere it's cited (KPI #3, abstract, literature comparison).

## D24 — demo_gui.m: interactive operator-console demonstration
**Decision:** build `demo_gui.m`, a self-contained MATLAB `uifigure` app for live, in-person demonstration of the full closed-loop pipeline — multi-select threat list (runs each selection in sequence), SNR control, live spectrogram and IQ-constellation (before/after mitigation), a GCS↔UAV link-status indicator that changes color with system state, CNN-confidence/latency gauges, a persistent run-history table exportable to CSV/.mat, and optional full-session video recording via `VideoWriter`. Every run calls the real pipeline (`build_threat_model.m`, `sim()`, `extract_closed_loop_frames.m`, the trained CNN and DQN, and — for any non-`no_action` decision — a second real simulation to measure actual post-mitigation BER) — nothing is mocked or precomputed.
**Architecture constraint discovered and resolved:** MATLAB nested functions (a function defined inside another) force the containing function's workspace to become "static," which then rejects script-style variable injection — this breaks any call to `init_params.m` or `build_threat_model.m` (both scripts, used as such everywhere else in the codebase) if they're invoked from a function that contains a nested function anywhere in the same file. `demo_gui.m` is therefore written with **no nested functions at all**: `demo_gui` builds the UI and stores every shared object (loaded models, parameters, UI component handles, run history, the video writer) in `fig.UserData`; every callback (`runSequence`, `runOneThreat`, `exportHistory`, etc.) is a plain top-level sibling function that retrieves what it needs via `ancestor(source,'figure').UserData`. This pattern should be reused for any future MATLAB GUI work in this codebase to avoid re-discovering the same restriction.
**Status:** built and iterated through two correctness passes (the static-workspace fix above, then two MATLAB API corrections — `uipanel` has no `FontColor` property, title-text color is `ForegroundColor`; and `'\u25CF'`-style escapes are not interpreted in MATLAB single-quoted strings, `char(9679)` must be used instead). Live-tested by Adi as of this session's end; further UI feedback pending.
**Superseded in part by D26 (2026-09-22):** the no-nested-functions architecture and `fig.UserData` pattern remain the standard; the feature set described above was replaced by the four-tab v3 console.

## D25 — UAV speed envelope 50–120 km/h (continuous) and speed-diverse training data
**Decision:** widen the UAV operating envelope from 18–22 m/s (D4) to a continuous 50–120 km/h (13.9–33.3 m/s), with Doppler fd = v·fc/c = 111–267 Hz at 2.4 GHz (≈ 2.22 Hz per km/h). Nominal stays 72 km/h (20 m/s, 160 Hz), so every script that does not sweep speed behaves as before. The detector is trained and evaluated on speed-diverse data, and a dedicated closed-loop sweep measures robustness across the envelope.
**Alternatives:** (a) keep the narrow D4 envelope — rejected: speed generalization would stay untested and the GUI speed control would remain cosmetic; (b) an integer km/h grid — rejected: the requirement is a continuous, non-integer speed; (c) feed speed to the network — rejected: a deployed receiver does not know the true speed, so robustness must come from the data (speed is stored per frame for analysis only); (d) also randomize the UAV–GCS distance — not required by the proposal, and path attenuation is already covered by the path_loss severity axis.
**Implementation:**
- `init_params.m` — `speed_kmh_min/max` (50/120), derived `v_min/v_max`, nominal unchanged.
- `run_dataset_sweep.m` — every 100-frame (threat, level, Eb/N0) block is simulated at its own random continuous speed, assigned by a Latin square over 6 speed bins so speed is correlated with neither SNR nor class; `none` is split into 5 sub-blocks per SNR so it also spans the speeds; per-frame `speed_kmh`; `rng(2026)`. The Doppler is baked into the Simulink model at build time, so this needs 270 builds (~102 min) instead of one per (threat, level).
- `extract_spectrograms.m` — a speed change starts a new run (prevents temporal-feature leakage across speeds); `prepare_data.m` carries `speed` in each split (analysis-only); `eval_detector.m` reports accuracy in 7 speed bins.
- `eval_speed_robustness.m` (new) — 8 speeds × 3 Eb/N0 × 9 threats through the real chain (Simulink → CNN → DQN → real mitigation re-simulation); ~13 min.
**Rationale:** the earlier system was trained and evaluated at a single Doppler (160 Hz); a reviewer can fairly ask whether it holds across realistic speeds. This makes the answer measurable. Consistent with D4, fading is deeply quasi-static across the whole envelope (normalized Doppler ≤ 2.7e-4 at 1 Msym/s), so mild speed sensitivity is expected; the sweep confirms it instead of assuming it. The upper end (33 m/s) now touches the 30–36 m/s cruise range that D4 had cited for larger platforms; the envelope is defined by speed rather than platform class, and the small-UAS link scope of D3 is unchanged.
**Result (2026-09-21 full re-run):** offline accuracy 96.41% (96.73% before), macro-F1 96.39%; per-speed offline accuracy 92.7–98.2% (weakest bin 50–60 km/h); closed-loop detection over 8 speeds 98.1% (212/216) with 0/48 false alarms and mean recovery 72.4% (flat at 69–74%); all four misses are antenna_fault. Side effect: reactive_jamming recall fell from 91.0% to 87.1% (untested hypothesis: extra Doppler variation blurs the temporal features that separate it from jamming).
**Scope limits:** the DQN state has no Doppler input and its reward table, EXP and SURV run at the nominal condition; the DQN was retrained in the full run but its training is speed-independent. Only detection and closed-loop behavior are speed-swept.

## D27 — KPI #2 measured against the no-attack (clean) link
**Decision:** report link recovery the way the approved proposal defines KPI #2 ("restoring BER / packet loss to a defined fraction of the no-attack values"): `rec = 100·(BER_before − BER_after)/(BER_before − BER_clean)`, capped at 100, together with the link state `BER_after/BER_clean` (≤ 2 restored, ≤ 5 marginal — the survivability-map thresholds). The previous metric, `100·(BER_before − BER_after)/BER_before`, is kept alongside for comparison.
**Problem it fixes:** the previous metric measured recovery relative to the attacked BER, not to the healthy link. It penalised threats that start from a low BER and hid whether the link actually returned to normal: sweeping_jammer scored 60.4% and noise_burst 71.6% although both return to ~1.1–1.2× the clean BER, and one GUI run showed "95% recovered — MARGINAL" next to "77% recovered — RECOVERABLE". The verdicts already used the clean reference; the percentage did not.
**Implementation:** `recovery_vs_clean.m` (shared function; NaN when the attack caused < 10% degradation). Clean reference = the `none` run at the same Eb/N0 (and speed) inside the same sweep; a real threat left at `no_action` is reported as a missed detection, not averaged in. Integrated into `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m` (new Section 0 and summary lines, recovery plot), `eval_speed_robustness.m`, `measure_all_kpis.m` (re-runs the diagnostic automatically if the saved results predate D27), `build_kpi_dashboard.m` and `demo_gui.m` (recovery vs the EXP clean BER; `ber_clean_ref` exported to CSV). `recompute_recovery_vs_clean.m` re-scores saved results without simulation.
**Validation:** the two independent clean references (closed-loop `none` runs vs EXP) agree within 2–15% at every Eb/N0. Re-scoring the 2026-09-21 run: 95.6% per-run mean (95.3% per-threat), 37/41 runs restored, 4 marginal, 0 not restored, 1 missed detection; speed sweep 94.7–96.9% at every speed (95.6% overall). Previous metric on the same runs: 76.3% / 72.4%. Native re-run inside `run_closed_loop_diagnostic` (2026-09-23): 96.2% per-run, 37/41 restored, 4 marginal (all at 10 dB), 1 missed.
**Limits:** the clean reference is one 20-frame draw per Eb/N0, so ratios at 8–10 dB (clean BER ~3e-3 to 9e-3) are the noisiest — Monte Carlo confidence intervals are planned. The recovery is measured within the modelled countermeasure strength (fixed `action_mitigation_db`); it does not validate real-world countermeasure magnitudes. The DQN reward still uses the previous metric until the DQN is retrained.

## D28 — Physics-based countermeasure model, one shared function
**Decision:** replace the fixed per-action dB table (`action_mitigation_db` = 25/15/25/25 dB, duplicated in 7 scripts and subtracted from each threat's own severity field) with `apply_countermeasure.m`, which applies each action's physical effect to the TRUE threat. Used by `train_dqn.m`, `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m`, `run_closed_loop_with_detector.m`, `eval_speed_robustness.m` and `demo_gui.m`.
**Problem it fixes:** under the old table three actions were interchangeable (12 of 28 DQN-vs-rule disagreements in a GUI session ended within 5% of each other), and effects were unphysical — a channel switch reduced path loss and antenna-fault attenuation by 25 dB. It also left a fixed residual that dominated at high Eb/N0 (all four marginal outcomes in the D27 run were at 10 dB). The proposal names the actions (channel switch, rate change, frequency/spatial diversity) but not their magnitudes, so the model is a design choice, stated here.
**Model:** threats grouped by spectrum occupancy — in-channel (jamming, reactive_jamming, spoofing, benign_interference), swept (sweeping_jammer), broadband (noise_burst), signal-side (path_loss, antenna_fault). `channel_switch`: in-channel interference −`cm_acr_db` (30 dB adjacent-channel rejection), nothing else. `freq_diversity`: in-channel −30 dB, swept duty → duty², 2× spectrum. `spatial_diversity`: MRC with `cm_n_rx` = 2, +3 dB against noise and spatially uncorrelated interference; antenna_fault — the healthy antenna replaces the faulty one. `rate_reduce`: rate ÷ `cm_rate_factor` = 4, +6 dB processing gain against noise and noise-like interference, none against the coherent spoofer, goodput × 0.25. Constants in `init_params.m`. The reactive jammer is modelled as monitoring our current channel (a jammer that follows the hop is the planned Dynamic/Chasing Jammer extension).
**Rule-based baseline:** aligned to the same physics — sweeping_jammer → `freq_diversity` (was `channel_switch_fast`, ineffective against a jammer that visits every channel), spoofing → `channel_switch` (was `freq_diversity`, same effect at half the spectrum); `channel_switch_fast` removed. A baseline that contradicts the system's own physics would make any DQN advantage artificial.
**Consequences:** results before D28 (C3, speed sweep, EXP/SURV, KPI #2 = 96.2%) are the pre-D28 reference. Recovery is expected to fall where no action addresses the threat (strong path_loss, broadband noise_burst at high Eb/N0) — these become genuine non-recoverable regimes, which proposal deliverable 7 asks to map. The DQN reward must include each action's cost (goodput, spectrum), otherwise `rate_reduce` becomes a universal booster — handled in the next step (reward, benign policy, retrain).
**Verification (real link, `results/countermeasure_matrix.txt`, 2026-09-23):** the physics behaves as specified — `channel_switch` leaves sweeping_jammer, noise_burst, path_loss and antenna_fault at their unmitigated BER (within ±4%, single draw), `rate_reduce` does nothing against spoofing, the healthy antenna restores antenna_fault to 1.00× clean. Best achievable link state (any action): jamming, reactive_jamming, spoofing and antenna_fault restored at 0/4/10 dB; sweeping_jammer restored at 0/4 dB, marginal at 10 dB (4.4×); noise_burst restored at 0/4 dB, **not restorable at 10 dB (29×)**; path_loss restored at 0 dB, marginal at 4 dB (2.6×), **not restorable at 10 dB (7.0×)** — the first genuine non-recoverable regimes, and the narrow (in-channel, fully recoverable) versus broadband (noise_burst) contrast of proposal deliverable 7. benign_interference with `no_action` reaches 25× clean at 10 dB (1.27× after a channel switch). Ignoring cost, `rate_reduce` gives the lowest BER in 9 of 21 real-threat cells and pushes the clean link to 0.06–0.18×, so the reward must price goodput. The rule-based choice is within 10% of the best BER in 18 of 21 cells; the other 3 are cells where `rate_reduce` is lower but the rule's cost-free action already restores the link. Consequence: in the one-shot setting a learned policy can at best match the rule on nominal threats; its measurable value lies in SNR-dependent choices (benign, noise_burst at high Eb/N0), detection errors and the episodic scenario (improvement 4).

## D29 — DQN reward with action costs, training over the full Eb/N0 range, FAR as "unnecessary action"
**Decision:** rebuild `train_dqn.m` on the D27 metric and the D28 physics. (1) The reward table is measured through the real link for every threat × action × Eb/N0 (9 × 5 × 6 = 270 simulations, 45 model builds), instead of at Eb/N0 = 0 dB only. (2) Reward of an action = link score − cost: score 100 if BER ≤ 1.15× clean (the draw-to-draw spread of the clean reference, D27), otherwise recovery vs clean; cost = 100·(0.30·goodput lost + 0.05·extra channels), i.e. `rate_reduce` (rate / 4) costs 22.5 points and `freq_diversity` 5. (3) On a non-hostile link that is NOT degraded (BER ≤ 2× clean) any action scores −40 (false alarm); a non-hostile link degraded beyond 2× is treated like a threat. (4) Episodes sample a (threat, Eb/N0) cell and a real per-frame link state from its measured run — no simulation inside the training loop — for 4,000 episodes; 10% of episodes hide the class (all-zero one-hot) so the agent also learns a link-metric-only fallback (proposal risk 13). (5) The DQN state carries log10(BER) instead of BER (`build_dqn_state.m`, the single source for training and inference). (6) The validation gate checks every (threat, Eb/N0) cell: hard failure for acting on `none` or on benign interference below 1.5× clean, or for `no_action` where acting is worth ≥ 30 points; all other cells are reported by regret. (7) Adam moments are carried across updates (previously reset every step).
**Why:** the D28 matrix showed that (a) the right decision depends on Eb/N0 — benign interference is 1.36× clean at 0 dB but 25× at 10 dB — so a policy trained at one Eb/N0 cannot learn it; (b) without a cost, `rate_reduce` gives the lowest BER in 9 of 21 real-threat cells and pushes even the clean link to 0.06×, so it would become a universal action. The proposal treats goodput as a trade-off against survivability, so survival dominates and cost breaks ties: checked on the measured matrix, `rate_reduce` is chosen only where it restores the link materially better than any free action (path_loss at every Eb/N0, noise_burst at 0–4 dB), and never when a free action already restores it (antenna_fault → spatial, sweeping_jammer → freq_diversity at 4–10 dB).
**FAR definition:** the proposal defines a false alarm as an unnecessary channel switch that disrupts the link. `diagnose_far_measurement.m` now counts an action as a false alarm only when the link is not degraded (BER ≤ 2× the clean trials at that Eb/N0); actions on degraded benign links are reported as justified, and the pre-D29 "any action" rate is still printed. This follows proposal mitigation 13 (respond to functional degradation, not to the threat's name).
**Result (2026-09-23):** training 7 min; average reward plateaus at ~62–66 (includes 10% class-hidden episodes); validation gate passed on all 54 cells, mean regret 1.5, maximum 11, none above 15. Closed loop (C3 diagnostic on the new agent): KPI #2 83.0% vs clean (30/42 restored, 8 marginal, 4 not restored, 0 missed detections) — lower than the pre-D28 96.2% because noise_burst (33.1%, 11× clean) and path_loss (63.4%, 6.7×) are now physically not fully restorable; in-channel threats, spoofing and antenna_fault 98.9–99.9%. FAR 0/120 on healthy-link trials; benign interference at 4 and 10 dB is degraded in 60/60 trials and was acted on in all of them (justified); any-action rate 33.3%. DQN-vs-rule agreement 66.7% (53.7% before).
**Limits:** the costs (0.30, 0.05) and the 1.15 tolerance are design choices, stated here and checked against the measured matrix, not optimized. One-shot decisions only; recovery time and hysteresis come with the episodic closed loop (improvement 4).

## D30 — Survivability map on the real action set; degradation-aware rule baseline
**Decision 1 — map:** `map_survivability_boundary.m` no longer reads the EXP mechanism study. It simulates every threat × severity level (the dataset's 5 levels) × Eb/N0 × action through `apply_countermeasure.m` (8 × 5 × 6 × 5 = 1,200 simulations, 200 builds, ~75 min) and classifies each cell by the best achievable BER vs the clean link (≤ 2× recoverable, ≤ 5× marginal). Map A = actions without goodput loss (channel_switch, freq_diversity, spatial_diversity); Map B = Map A + rate_reduce. Gap cells (Map B survives, Map A does not) are the proposal's goodput trade-off regime; every cell also records which action achieves the best link. Output files and the `data/survivability_boundary.mat` structure are unchanged (plus `best_action`, `ber_all`), so the GUI and dashboard read it as before; labels updated. `explore_countermeasures.m` is kept as the pre-D28 mechanism study, off by default in `main.m`.
**Why:** the previous maps were built from abstract mechanisms and magnitudes (attenuation/field reduction, AWGN margin), not from the actions the system can take, so "recoverable" did not mean "recoverable by this system". Deliverable 7 asks where an attack is recoverable; with D28 that question has a direct, action-level answer.
**Decision 2 — rule baseline:** `rule_based_policy.m` takes the observed BER and Eb/N0 (optional arguments) and, when the class maps to `no_action` (none, benign_interference, unknown) but the link is degraded beyond 2× the clean BER (`clean_ber_ref.m`), applies a generic action — `channel_switch` for benign interference, `freq_diversity` otherwise. The proposal requires this of both the agent and the rules (mitigation 13: react to functional degradation, not only to the threat's name); in the D29 run the rule left degraded benign links untouched while the DQN acted, which made 4 of 6 benign disagreements a rule defect rather than a DQN advantage. Called with the same last-frame observation the DQN receives (C3 diagnostic, GUI; the GUI's UNKNOWN path now also uses it).
**Result (2026-09-23, 78 min):** Map A (without goodput loss) 78.8% recoverable / 9.6% marginal / 11.7% non-recoverable; Map B (any action) 80.8 / 10.0 / 9.2% (240 states each; pre-D28 mechanism maps were 85.5 / 88.3% recoverable). Per threat (A / B recoverable): spoofing, antenna_fault and benign 100 / 100%, reactive_jamming 97 / 97%, jamming 93 / 93%, sweeping_jammer 77 / 77% (non-recoverable only at 10 dB, levels 12–16), noise_burst 43 / 50%, path_loss 20 / 30%. The proposal's narrow-versus-broadband contrast appears directly: in-channel interferers are recoverable at every severity by leaving or diversifying the channel, while broadband noise_burst is non-recoverable from 6–10 dB even at its lowest severity, and strong path loss is non-recoverable because no single action repairs the link budget. 6 gap cells (noise_burst 2, path_loss 4), all survived only through rate reduction.
**Evaluation fixes found in this run:** (a) `eval_speed_robustness.m` still counted any action on a non-hostile class as a false alarm; its 2/6 "false alarms" at every speed were exactly the justified actions on benign interference at 4 and 10 dB. It now uses the D29 definition and reports justified actions separately. (b) `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m` fed the rule the ground-truth threat while the DQN received the detector output, and measured only the DQN's resulting link. The rule now receives the same detected class and link measurements, its chosen action is simulated as well, and the report compares recovery, link ratio and goodput of both policies on the same runs — the link-quality half of proposal KPI #3; `measure_kpi3_recovery_time.m` shows both. Agreement figures before this change are not comparable.

## D31 — Episodic closed loop: recovery time in decision cycles, dwell/hysteresis
**Decision:** add `run_closed_loop_episodes.m`, which measures KPI #3 as the proposal words it — recovery time in decision cycles/frames and the resulting link quality, DQN versus rule-based — and implements the dwell/hysteresis promised in mitigation 8. An episode runs 20 clean cycles, the threat starts, and 60 cycles follow. Each cycle is one received frame: the trained CNN classifies it (new shared `detect_frame.m`, same preprocessing as the C3 diagnostic, temporal features over the episode's own history), the policy proposes an action, and an action is a persistent configuration change (`no_action` keeps the current one). A new action is committed only after 3 consecutive proposals and not within 10 cycles of the previous switch. Frames are drawn from real Simulink runs of every (threat, action, Eb/N0) — 2 runs per cell, 270 in total — so after a countermeasure the detector sees the mitigated link. Metrics from onset: T_detect (first correct class), T_act (first committed countermeasure), T_recover (first cycle from which the 5-cycle BER average stays ≤ 2× clean for 10 cycles), switches, false switches on healthy links, final BER/clean, goodput. Both policies, with and without hysteresis, on identical random frame draws; 9 threats × Eb/N0 {0, 4, 10} × 5 episodes. `measure_kpi3_recovery_time.m` reports the result; `main.m` flag `run_closed_loop_episodes`.
**Why:** the one-shot evaluation can only compare which action each policy picks; it cannot show how long the loop takes to react, whether misdetections cause flip-flopping, or what the loop does after the countermeasure changes the link it is observing (risk 8, closed-loop instability). The earlier re-definition of KPI #3 as decision latency is superseded; latency stays as a component.
**Time base:** a cycle is one frame of signal (0.516 ms). The measured decision latency (~11 ms) exceeds a frame, so a real-time loop would decide about every 22 frames; recovery times are reported in cycles (the proposal's unit) with this conversion stated.
**First run (2026-09-24, 540 episodes):** hysteresis is decisive — DQN switches per episode 18.5 → 1.5, rule 7.4 → 1.5; healthy-link episodes with a false switch 14/20 → 1/20 for both; pre-onset switches DQN 58 → 0, rule 99 → 3. Without hysteresis the DQN is far more jittery than the rule, because it reacts to every noisy per-frame state. With hysteresis both commit the first countermeasure after a median of 3 cycles (the dwell) and recover in a median of 8 cycles (~90 ms at the measured ~11 ms decision latency); DQN recovered 89/115 episodes needing action vs rule 92/115, with goodput 0.92 vs 0.85 and fewer multi-switch episodes (40 vs 55).
**Metric correction:** the first report counted T_recover = 1 for episodes whose link never left the 2× band (threats that barely degrade the link at 0 dB) and for episodes where a false switch before onset had already mitigated the threat — the "median T_recover 1 without hysteresis" was this artefact. `report_closed_loop_episodes.m` now classifies every episode from its saved trace as recovered / not recovered / not degraded / pre-configured and times recovery only for degraded, not pre-configured episodes (no re-simulation needed).
**Detection at onset (proposal risk 7):** exact-class detection is often late: noise_burst is first labelled correctly about 10 cycles after onset — the length of the temporal-feature window — and jamming at 0–4 dB is never labelled exactly within the episode, yet both policies act after 3 cycles and recover. The detector was trained without windows spanning a threat's start (mitigation 7 excluded them), so at onset the temporal features are out of distribution; the early labels are wrong but mostly map to the same countermeasure (jamming ↔ reactive_jamming → channel_switch). Candidate follow-up in the detector-quality step: train with transition windows, or report T_act/T_recover rather than exact-class latency as the operational measure.
**Corrected results (2026-09-24, re-scored from the saved traces):** with hysteresis both policies commit after a median of 3 cycles and recover in a median of 8 cycles (~35 ms and ~90 ms at the measured decision rate). Of the episodes the threat actually degraded, the DQN recovered 61/87 (70%) and the rule 68/91 (75%); goodput 0.92 vs 0.85; 0 vs 3 switches before onset. The rule's lead comes from noise_burst at 4 dB (rate_reduce, 3/5 vs 0/5), as in the one-shot comparison. Episodes the DQN acted on so early that the link never crossed 2× clean ("not degraded": DQN 28, rule 21 — e.g. antenna_fault and sweeping_jammer at 4 dB) are a small speed advantage, not missing data. Non-recovered cells (sweeping_jammer 10 dB, noise_burst 4–10 dB, path_loss 4–10 dB) coincide with the survivability map's marginal / non-recoverable regimes at nominal severity. Without hysteresis the loop is not usable: 47 (DQN) and 52 (rule) of 115 episodes already had a false countermeasure running when the threat began, and of the genuinely degraded episodes only 18/43 (DQN) and 16/32 (rule) recovered.
**Verification:** stub-tested end to end before the MATLAB run.

## D32 — Unknown-threat detection (leave-one-threat-out) and combined threats
**Decision:** evaluate proposal deliverable 4 ("classify ... or detect an anomaly / unknown threat") and risk 13 (a new or combined threat not in the dataset leading to a wrong or frozen decision) with two new evaluations, and give the system a calibrated unknown-threat threshold.
- **Scores and threshold.** Two standard out-of-distribution scores from the detector's own outputs: maximum softmax probability (Hendrycks & Gimpel, ICLR 2017) and energy = logsumexp of the logits (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020), read from layer `fc_out` (`cnn_scores.m`, `ood_scores.m`). The unknown threshold keeps 95% of known-class validation frames (`ood_thresholds.m`, cached in `data/ood_thresholds.mat`); the GUI's UNKNOWN slider now starts at that value.
- **`eval_ood_detection.m` (leave-one-threat-out).** For each of the 8 threat classes the detector is retrained from scratch without it (same architecture, split and schedule as `train_detector.m`); that class then plays the unknown threat on the test split. Reported per class: AUROC of both scores, share of unknown frames flagged at the 95% threshold, false flags on known frames, the class the unknown threat is mistaken for, and whether that mistake still leads to the right countermeasure. ~8 trainings, ~60 min; off by default in `main.m`.
- **`eval_combined_threats.m`.** Four combinations absent from the dataset, each component at nominal severity — jamming+path_loss (in-channel + signal-side), noise_burst+antenna_fault (broadband + signal-side), sweeping_jammer+path_loss (swept + signal-side), spoofing+noise_burst (coherent + broadband) — plus the clean link as control, at 0 / 4 / 10 dB. Every frame is classified and scored; four policies decide on it — DQN and rule, each with the detected class or with unknown gating (below threshold → class `unknown`: the DQN gets an all-zero one-hot, the rule reacts to link degradation only); every action is simulated on every combination, so each decision is scored by the link it actually produces. ~10 min.
**Model support:** `build_threat_model.m` builds a combined threat `'a+b'` by composing each component's model and parameters with its own persistent state, signal-side components first so they attenuate only our signal; the single-threat code is unchanged (verified line by line), so the dataset and detector are unaffected. `apply_countermeasure.m` applies an action to each component, counts the action's Eb/N0 gain once, lets spatial diversity only replace the faulty antenna when an antenna fault is present, and rejects combinations whose components share a severity field (e.g. two jammers on `jsr_db`).
**Refactor:** the frame preprocessing is now one function (`frame_features.m`) used by `detect_frame.m` and the combined-threat evaluation.
**Verification so far:** all new code executed end to end against stubbed Simulink, detector and DQN; the combined-threat Simulink scripts were rendered and inspected; `apply_countermeasure.m` checked for every combination × action. The MATLAB runs are pending.
**Results (2026-09-24):** leave-one-threat-out mean AUROC MSP 0.515, energy 0.521; per class MSP / energy: benign 0.901 / 0.968, antenna_fault 0.791 / 0.854, path_loss 0.727 / 0.548, sweeping_jammer 0.688 / 0.493, spoofing 0.639 / 0.912, reactive_jamming 0.183 / 0.158, jamming 0.159 / 0.208, noise_burst 0.034 / 0.029. AUROC below 0.5 means the unknown threat scores *higher* than known frames: it is absorbed, confidently, into its closest sibling (jamming ↔ reactive_jamming, noise_burst → sweeping_jammer). At the 95% threshold 16% (MSP) / 23% (energy) of unknown frames are flagged, with 5% false flags. The mistake is harmless for jamming/reactive (93–100% same countermeasure) and harmful for noise_burst, path_loss, spoofing, antenna_fault and sweeping_jammer (0% same action). Combined threats: detected as their dominant component with MSP 0.92–1.00, never flagged unknown; no decision was no_action on an attack; the best single action restores only 2 of 12 (combination, Eb/N0) cells — the DQN's decisions reached both, the rule's one (noise_burst+antenna_fault at 0 dB via rate_reduce); frames within a cell choose the same action, so the effective sample is 12 cells, not 456 frames. Control: 68% of clean-link frames at 0 dB fell below the MSP threshold, and with confidence-only gating the DQN acted on the clean link in 28/114 frames (4/114 without gating).
**Conclusion:** maximum-softmax and energy scores do not detect near-duplicate or combined threats in this system; they separate only threats with a distinct signature. The system's protection against an unknown threat is its response to link degradation (mitigation 13) and the fact that many confusions map to the same countermeasure. Candidate improvement: feature-space scores (Mahalanobis distance on the penultimate layer), outlier exposure.

## D33 — Unknown gating requires a degraded link; file consolidation

## D35 — KPI reporting as worded (PLR, goodput, FAR bound) and Monte Carlo confidence intervals
**Decision:** close improvement steps 7 and 8 of the plan. Report every KPI as the proposal words it, and give the main numbers 95% confidence intervals from independent repeats.
- **Packet loss (KPI #2: "BER / packet loss restored to a defined share of the no-attack values"):** a frame (packet) is lost when its BER > 0.1, the definition already used by the detector's PLR feature (no FEC is modelled). PLR is restored when PLR_after ≤ PLR_clean + 0.05. An absolute tolerance is used because PLR_clean is 0 at most Eb/N0, so a ratio is undefined. PLR recovery = 100·(before − after)/(before − clean), NaN when the attack adds < 0.02 loss.
- **Goodput kept** = goodput factor of the action × (1 − PLR_after)/(1 − PLR_clean), the share of no-attack throughput. It is reported for DQN, rule and no action, as the survivability-vs-goodput trade-off the proposal names.
- **FAR bound (KPI #4: "below a defined bound for SNR above a threshold"):** the proposal does not give the number. Bound set here: FAR ≤ 5% with the 95% Wilson upper limit below 5%, pooled over healthy-link trials at Eb/N0 ≥ the KPI #1 threshold. The 5% matches the dashboard's existing flag. Per-Eb/N0 values are listed with intervals; a single Eb/N0 with 60 trials cannot reach a 5% upper limit even at 0 events (6.0%), so the verdict uses the pooled value.
- **Monte Carlo (closed loop):** `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m` repeats every cell `CFG.mc_repeats` (5) times. Seeds: `rng(seed)` plus the AWGN and bit-source block seeds, set per build. Parameter names are matched case-insensitively among the dialog parameters, because they differ between releases. Each run's before/after/rule sims share the seed (common random numbers, paired comparison); repeats and cells use different seeds. The clean reference is the `none` run of the same repeat and Eb/N0. Headline values: mean over repeats with a Student-t 95% interval over per-repeat values (tabulated quantiles, no toolbox). Pooled proportions: Wilson. DQN − rule is computed per repeat, so its interval is a paired test. If repeats come out identical, the report prints a warning instead of a zero-width interval.
- **Detector:** 1,000-resample bootstrap of the test set for accuracy, macro-F1 and macro-F1 above the threshold (local stream, global RNG untouched). Retraining variance is already measured by the eight LOTO retrainings (D32); no further detector retraining.
- **DQN seeds:** `train_dqn.m` trains `CFG.dqn_seeds` (5) agents on the same measured reward table. Every agent goes through the validation gate; the saved one is the gate-passing seed with the lowest mean regret (tie-break: max regret). The spread across seeds and the cells where seeds disagree go to `results/dqn_seed_stability.txt`. This answers whether the weak cell (path_loss at 10 dB) is a seed effect.
- **Consumers:** `measure_all_kpis.m` (CIs, PLR, goodput, FAR bound, episode intervals, DQN seeds, detector bootstrap), `build_kpi_dashboard.m` (means and error bars over repeats, FAR upper limit), `demo_gui.m` KPI tab (the D27 metric instead of the pre-D27 `recovery_pct`, means over repeats, CIs). New shared helper `stats_ci.m`. `main.m`: `CFG` block, steps 7+8 preset, the fixed CHECKPOINT numbers removed.
**Why a new agent means re-running the DQN consumers:** the selected seed is a different network from the current agent, so the diagnostic, episodes, combined threats, speed sweep and FAR are re-run in the same preset. The survivability map does not use the DQN.
**Verification:** block/bracket balance checked on every changed file; no MATLAB available here, so the MATLAB run is the test.

## D36 — DQN trained on full-action targets; gate rejects regret > 10
**Finding (D35 run):** DQN − rule recovery −4.0 [−5.7, −2.4] points, significant. The gap is path_loss: 36.4% vs 69.0%, 0/30 vs rule 33% PLR-restored, 0% agreement. Every one of the 5 seeds chose spatial_diversity for path_loss; the reward table prefers rate_reduce there, and 4 seeds did the same on noise_burst at 0–2 dB (regret 10–15). The agent had learned a worse action than its own reward prescribes. This is not the goodput trade-off built into the reward.
**Cause:** the Q-network was updated only for the action it had just taken. rate_reduce is the costly action and its margin over spatial_diversity is 10–15 reward points; once epsilon decays, the greedy choice stops sampling it and its Q-value stays under-estimated. The gate only failed missed actions worth ≥ 30 points, so regret 10–15 passed.
**Decision:**
- Every (threat, action, Eb/N0) reward is already measured through the real link (270 simulations). Each sampled state therefore trains all five Q-values toward their table rewards (full-action targets, loss over all outputs). The epsilon-greedy choice remains only to log the reward the policy collects. It is still a Q-network with one-step reward targets on real per-frame states (10% with the class hidden); what is removed is the dependence on exploration luck.
- Gate: a cell that needs action also fails when regret > 10. Seed failures are listed in `dqn_seed_stability.txt`.
- Confidence intervals are clipped to the metric's range (a 95.6% mean with n = 5 showed an upper limit of 104.9%).
**Expected:** regret ≈ 0 on most cells, rate_reduce for path_loss and for noise_burst where the table prefers it, DQN − rule interval containing 0, the DQN goodput advantage shrinking (it now pays for rate_reduce where the reward says it is worth it).
**Result (2026-09-25):** all 5 seeds pass, mean regret 0.1, the selected agent is reward-optimal in every cell. DQN − rule +1.0 [−1.6, 3.6] points (was −4.0 [−5.7, −2.4]); path_loss 36.4% → 69.0%; goodput kept DQN 78.4% vs rule 74.7%. The DQN is now at least as good as the rule on link quality, keeps more throughput, and makes no false switches on healthy links in the episodic loop (rule 3/20).

## D39 — Action set v2: transmit power control, FEC with interleaving, two-action pairs
**Why:** the D38 full run left three regimes weak: noise_burst (51.1% recovery, 11/30 restored), path_loss (69.0%, 5/30) and combined threats (38/456). No action in the set addressed their physics — the literature review (2026-09-25) names the missing responses: burst errors are handled with interleaved error-correcting codes (Proakis & Salehi), jamming and weak links with transmit power control (Yang et al. 2025, "confrontation" strategy), and a single action cannot repair two impairments at once. The approved proposal already lists "change of transmission rate" and "Adaptive Link Recovery"; the addendum submitted with the corrected proposal form names the three extensions explicitly.
**Decision:**
- `power_control`: +6 dB transmit power (`cm_power_db`): Eb/N0 +6 dB and every additive interferer −6 dB relative to the signal (spoofer included, unlike rate reduction); cost ×4 power, 10 reward points.
- `fec_interleave`: rate-1/2 convolutional code, K = 7, generators [171 133] octal, random interleaver over the whole run, Viterbi decoding (`vitdec`, unquantized) with erasures on symbols whose received energy is > 6 dB above the run's median (dilated ±3 symbols for filter delay). Emulated on the measured channel error pattern of the same run: the channel symbols are unchanged (same symbol rate and energy), so Eb/N0 is not touched and goodput halves. Erasures are derived from the received signal, not from the threat's known schedule.
- Pairs: {channel_switch, freq_diversity, spatial_diversity} × {rate_reduce, power_control, fec_interleave} — 9 pairs; Eb/N0 gains add, goodput and power multiply, spectrum takes the larger factor. With no_action and six singles the agent has 16 actions.
- Rule baseline unchanged from D38 (noise_burst → rate_reduce). A first run with noise_burst → fec_interleave dropped the rule to −44.9% on noise_burst: below Eb/N0 ≈ 6 dB the burst-corrupted stream is past the code's threshold and Viterbi decoding makes the BER worse than uncoded. A fixed textbook rule would need an Eb/N0-dependent choice, which is what the DQN learns; keeping the D38 rule keeps the baseline as strong as before. The DQN keeps the full action set.
- Survivability maps: Map A = actions without goodput loss (C, F, S, power control and their pairs); Map B = every action.
- Consumers made action-count independent: GUI Q-value plot and action chart, dashboard action panel, link-diagram drawing (pairs drawn as the first action plus a label).
- `run_closed_loop_diagnostic.m` now extracts the post-action frames with the post-action parameters (needed for FEC; for the earlier actions the two were identical in the extractor).
- DQN fit for 16 actions: the first run failed the gate on every seed (mean regret 2.2–3.4, max 17–29). The reward table was sound; the misses were near-ties, mostly "X" vs "X + FEC" (15 points apart when both restore the link), within the network's fit error. Changes: input layer z-scores the continuous inputs with the training-state statistics (log10 BER, RSSI in dBm and Eb/N0 were on very different scales), hidden layers 64-32 → 128-64, 4,000 → 12,000 episodes, batch 32 → 64, learning rate constant for the first half then cosine decay to a tenth. The gate is unchanged (regret ≤ 10).
**Expected:** noise_burst restored at most Eb/N0 by fec_interleave or a pair with it; path_loss improved by power_control or rate_reduce+power pairs; combined threats restored more often through pairs; the DQN diverging from the rule where a pair beats a single action. Runtimes grow with the 16 actions (reward table 864 simulations, survivability map 3,840).
**Verification:** block/bracket balance on every changed file; MATLAB run pending (the FEC path uses `poly2trellis`, `convenc`, `vitdec`, Communications Toolbox).

## D41 — Multi-antenna UAV receiver, coherent combining, seeded link
**Why:** the review of 2026-09-26 found (1) the receiver never corrected the channel phase, so the clean-link BER sat 1.3–2 dB above theory (D7 kept this as a "documented degradation"; for a communications examiner it reads as a receiver defect); (2) the fading, the threat waveforms and the AWGN were identical in every build (no seeds reached them), so the whole dataset shared one fading realization; (3) `spatial_diversity` was a fixed +3 dB with no antennas in the model.
**Decision:**
- Modeled link: GCS → UAV command uplink; the receiver and the detector are on the UAV. The UAV side is the exposed one — omni antennas see a jammer from any direction, while the GCS can use a directional antenna — and losing the uplink means losing control. The GCS is modeled as one transmit antenna whose gain is part of Eb/N0, so no GCS array is needed.
- UAV: `n_rx` = 2 omni dipoles under the fuselage (V-mount, ≥ λ/2 apart), the configuration of the 2×2 datalink radios used on small UAVs; the code supports 3. Array model: ULA, spacing 0.5 λ, GCS at broadside, interferers at 40°, −55°, 70° (components 1–3 of a threat).
- Channel per antenna: LoS steering vector + diffuse Rayleigh (`comm.MIMOChannel`, Jakes, fd from the UAV speed), K = 10 dB, diffuse correlation 0.3 between adjacent antennas. Eb/N0 is per antenna.
- Threats: every additive threat is one waveform reaching each antenna through its own Rician spatial channel (so it can be nulled); path_loss scales all antennas; antenna_fault hits antenna 1 only. Waveforms, powers and schedules unchanged.
- Receiver: RRC per antenna; per window, least-squares channel estimate from the transmitted symbols (ideal pilots) and the covariance of the residual (interference + noise). Baseline `mrc` (w = h, 64-symbol window). `spatial_diversity` action → `mmse` (w = R⁻¹h, 32-symbol window): nulls up to n_rx − 1 interferers from other directions and follows a faulty branch; no Eb/N0 offset in `apply_countermeasure.m`. The detector input stays antenna 1's received IQ (same shape as before).
- Seeds: `p.seed` (or one draw from the global stream per build) seeds the signal channel, each interferer channel, the threat waveforms, the AWGN and the bit source. Scripts that call `rng(seed)` before building get common random numbers; scripts that do not get a new realization per build.
**Validation (`validate_phy.m`, 2026-09-26, 0.5 s per point):** Eb/N0 gap to closed-form BER (berawgn; MGF form, Simon & Alouini) — AWGN −0.05 dB, Rician SISO −0.06 dB, MRC 2 antennas −0.13 dB, MRC 3 antennas −0.17 dB, default link (ρ = 0.3) +0.04 dB: 5/5 within 0.3 dB (the old 1.3–2 dB offset is gone). Jamming 10 dB from 40°: MRC stays at BER 0.26; MMSE lowers BER by 6.8 dB (0 dB) to 30 dB (10 dB) and stays 2–3× above the jammer-free link at 0–2 dB, 10–230× at 6–10 dB — with two antennas the only degree of freedom goes to the null, so diversity is lost (physics, not a defect; input for the survivability map).
**Seed fix:** the Bernoulli bit source had `SeedSource = Auto`, so its Seed was ignored and the bits changed on every run (also in the D35/D40 seeded evaluations: AWGN was seeded, bits were not, so their pairing was partial). All seeding helpers now set `SeedSource = Parameter`. `diag_seeds.m`: same seed → identical bits, Tx IQ, channel, channel + AWGN and decisions, both on a repeated sim and after a rebuild.
**Consequences:** every stored dataset, detector, reward table and result predates D41 and must be regenerated (phase 2–4 of the plan). Amends D7 (coherent detection now in scope) and D28 (spatial_diversity physics). Idealizations stated for the report: symbols of the estimation window treated as known (upper bound of a pilot-aided receiver, pilot overhead not charged); no airframe shadowing; interferer directions fixed.

## D42 — Detector v2: fast seeded channel, run-level split, receiver-side features, unknown-threat scoring
**Why:** the 2026-09-26 review found train/test leakage (frames of one run on both sides), the configured Eb/N0 used as a detector input (a receiver does not know it), reactive_jamming at 82.8% recall (confused with jamming), jamming spectrograms saturating the fixed dB scale, the last frame of every run entering training with BER = 0, six copies of the feature code, and unknown-threat AUROC 0.52. The D41 validation run also showed that a build with `comm.MIMOChannel` in a MATLAB Function block compiled for ~1 min, and every new seed or speed was a new build.
**Decision:**
- Channel fading: sum of 32 sinusoids per antenna (random Doppler angles and phases, Jakes spectrum), Cholesky receive correlation; seed and Doppler enter the Channel and Threat blocks through Constant blocks, set by `link_seed.m` without a rebuild. Compiled block code is reused across seeds and speeds. Re-validated by `validate_phy.m`.
- Dataset (`run_dataset_sweep.m`): every (class, level, Eb/N0) cell = 5 independent sub-runs × 20 frames, own seed and own random speed (50–120 km/h); 41 builds instead of 270. Incomplete frames dropped.
- Split (`prepare_data.m`): sub-runs 1–3 train, 4 validation, 5 test. No sub-run crosses splits; every cell is in every split. Bootstrap intervals in `eval_detector.m` resample sub-runs.
- Features (`link_features.m`, one definition everywhere): [SINR estimate, BER, RSSI, PLR, var RSSI, dBER/dt, burst ratio, envelope correlation]. SINR = data-aided LS estimate on antenna 1 (replaces the configured Eb/N0). Envelope correlation = corr(|residual|², |own waveform|²) after a 32-sample LS fit: ~0 for interference independent of our transmission, ~0.3 for a jammer triggered by it (symbol-level check: 0.29 ± 0.02 vs |0.02| for all other threats, JSR 0–16 dB). Configured Eb/N0 is kept for analysis only.
- Image (`spec_image.m`): fixed scale [−40, 40] dB (the 20 dB ceiling clipped JSR ≥ ~6 dB).
- Training (`train_hybrid_net.m`, shared with the leave-one-threat-out study): cosine learning-rate decay, L2 1e-4, time/frequency masking augmentation (SpecAugment), best epoch by validation accuracy.
- Unknown threats (`fit_ood_model.m`, `ood_scores.m`): Mahalanobis distance on the 64-d embedding with a tied, shrunk covariance (Lee et al. 2018); isolation forest on the link features (Liu et al. 2008); fused = the lower of the two standardized scores. `eval_ood_detection.m` reports AUROC and FPR at 95% known retained for MSP, energy, Mahalanobis, isolation forest and fused, with the features re-normalized on the known classes. Production gating stays MSP until these results are in.
- Evaluation scripts seed the channel through `link_seed` (common random numbers within a repeat); pool runs that repeat `sim` reseed every run.
- Receiver: the channel estimate of each symbol uses the other symbols of its window (leave-one-out). With the symbol itself included, its own noise leaked into its estimate and made the BER ~2–4% optimistic.
- `validate_phy.m`: theory checks run at fd = 1 kHz with 20 channel realizations per point (~2400 independent fades; at 160 Hz and 0.5 s only ~190, and the single-antenna BER — set by rare deep fades — drifted ±0.4 dB between runs). BER theory is Doppler-independent while fd × CSI window ≪ 1 (1 kHz × 64 µs = 0.064). V5 is checked against the correlated-branch MGF. Pass: |gap| ≤ 0.3 dB or within 2 batch-means standard errors.
**Consequences:** detector inputs changed (8 features, new image scale): the dataset, detector, thresholds and everything downstream must be regenerated. The DQN still receives the configured Eb/N0 in its state (phase 3).

## D43 — Interference-over-thermal feature
**Why:** the D42 detector (test accuracy 90.9%, macro-F1 91.1%, reactive_jamming 100%) confused none (72.0%) with benign_interference (74.0%). Benign interference is modelled as weak white noise, so on the spectrogram and in the SINR estimate it is indistinguishable from a lower Eb/N0 — the D41 detector separated them only because it was given the configured Eb/N0, which D42 removed.
**Decision:** add interference over thermal (IoT, `iot_db.m`): residual power (RSSI / (1 + SINR), antenna 1) over the receiver's thermal noise floor. The floor is a receiver calibration constant (kTB × noise figure, the quantity 3GPP receivers report as IoT); in the normalized simulation it is the AWGN noise power of the run. ~0 dB on a clean link, rises with any external signal. Ninth link feature; computed from stored fields, so the D42 dataset is reused.
**Unknown-threat result (D42 detector, leave-one-threat-out):** mean AUROC MSP 0.608, energy 0.611, Mahalanobis 0.840, isolation forest 0.592, fused 0.794; FPR at 95% known kept: Mahalanobis 0.43. Mahalanobis ≥ 0.90 for jamming, noise_burst, reactive_jamming, path_loss, sweeping_jammer; weak for benign_interference (0.49 — physically close to none) and antenna_fault (0.74; energy 0.93).
**Result (2026-09-26, test split by sub-run, 5400 frames):** accuracy 96.24% [94.78, 97.46], macro-F1 96.26% [94.74, 97.41]; macro-F1 ≥ 90% from 0 dB (90.5% at 0 dB, 96.8–98.0% at 2–10 dB); none 97.3% (D42: 72.0%), benign_interference 94.0% (74.0%), reactive_jamming 100%, jamming 91.0% (errors mostly → reactive_jamming, same action); accuracy 94.7–98.3% in every 10 km/h speed bin. Mean IoT per class: none 0.0 dB, path_loss 0.0, antenna_fault 1.2, benign 4.5, spoofing 5.9, sweeping 8.8, noise_burst 11.2, reactive 14.7, jamming 15.9. ## D44 — Sequential decision layer: measured environment, Double DQN, follower jammer, escalation, baselines
**Why:** the D39–D40 agent was a contextual bandit (one decision per sample against a reward table, no target network, no replay, γ without effect), did not know its own configuration (so it could flip actions), was trained on the true class while deployed on the detector's output, and reached 26.5% on combined threats against 50% for the best single action. Its only comparison was the rule.
**Decision:**
- Environment (`link_env.m`) on measured frame pools (`build_policy_pools.m`): every scenario × configuration × Eb/N0 (0:2:10 dB) through the real link, detector applied (class probabilities, Mahalanobis score, 9 link features). Train split: 3 seeded sub-runs per cell, single threats + clean link. Test split: 2 other sub-runs per cell, plus 4 combined threats never used in training. Common random numbers across configurations. No Simulink inside learning.
- Episodes: 30 decision cycles (one frame each), clean until an onset at cycle 3–10. A follower jammer (in-channel threats, 50% of training episodes) re-acquires our channel 2–5 cycles after every hop; a configuration containing channel_switch then acts without it until the agent hops again.
- State (`policy_state.m`, 32): detector class probabilities, unknown flag (Mahalanobis below the 95% validation threshold), log BER, SINR, IoT, PLR, degradation vs the clean BER at the receiver's own Eb/N0 estimate (SINR + IoT = signal over thermal), current configuration (one-hot 16), cycles since the last change. The configured Eb/N0 is not an input.
- Reward per cycle: link quality 100 at ≤ 1.15× clean down to 0 at the unmitigated BER (log-linear), minus goodput/spectrum/power costs (as D29) and 2 points for adaptive combining, 5 points per change or hop, 20 for a change on a healthy link.
- Agent (`dqn_agent.m`, `train_dqn.m`): Double DQN with experience replay (150k), hard target update every 500 updates, Huber loss, γ = 0.9, ε 1 → 0.05, 12,000 episodes, 3 seeds; selection by validation return; gate: return ≥ rule + escalation and false switches on ≤ 5% of clean episodes. Ablation: the same agent with γ = 0 (contextual bandit).
- Policies (`policy_decide.m`, one function for training, evaluation and deployment): rule (class → action, dwell 2 / hold 3), escalation (link still degraded 3 cycles after a change → next configuration not yet tried in this incident: next-highest Q for the DQN, a fixed ladder for the rule), DQN, random, best fixed configuration, oracle (true scenario and follower state, best next-cycle reward). Unknown-threat gating uses the Mahalanobis score (D43 result).
- Evaluation (`evaluate_policies.m`): test pools, sets single / follower / combined (unseen) / clean, 95% t-intervals over episodes, paired DQN − rule differences.
**Consequences:** `build_dqn_state.m` and the one-shot consumers (closed-loop diagnostic, speed, FAR, combined, GUI) still use the D39 agent interface and are migrated to `policy_decide.m` in the next step; their flags stay off until then.

## D45 — Decision layer v2: flight geometry, reward floor, shield, fair baselines
**Why:** the first D44 run (2026-09-26) showed four problems. (1) The clean BER at Eb/N0 = 10 dB measured 0 (62k bits), so the reward reference was 0 and every configuration scored q = 0 there: one sixth of the episodes carried no quality signal, and the oracle capped at 83.3% restored and 0.833 clean return (= 5/6). (2) `since` started at 0 in every episode, so the state carried the episode clock; with onsets at cycle 3–10 the agent learned to switch before the threat (≈ 1 false switch per episode, 52% of clean validation episodes, gate FAIL). (3) Every interferer arrived from a fixed direction (40°), so adaptive combining nulled it in every episode: an always-on spatial_diversity + power_control beat every policy but the oracle and made the follower jammer irrelevant. (4) Frames were drawn from a random sub-run after each configuration change, so an episode did not keep one channel realization. Also: path_loss (10 dB) had no configuration able to recover it (best single gain 6 dB), and the rule was the only class-based baseline.
**Decision:**
- Geometry: interferer directions drawn per seed, uniform broadside angle in [−90°, 90°] (`interferer_aoa.m`; equal in distribution to a uniform azimuth around the UAV). The 'AoA' Constant block of the threat model (its UserData holds the mode and range) is set by `link_seed.m`, so no rebuild per sub-run; `init_params.int_aoa_random = true`. `validate_phy` keeps the fixed 40° for its V6 nulling test. The dataset, detector and pools are rebuilt with it.
- Pools: 6 train and 4 test sub-runs (geometries) per (scenario, Eb/N0), directions stored per frame. An episode stays in one sub-run; after a change it continues in the same sub-run at the same frame position (common random numbers across configurations). Reward, restored and healthy come from the configuration's mean BER in that sub-run; the oracle knows the sub-run.
- Reward reference: clean = max(clean-link BER, 1e-4); below 1e-4 the pools (≥ 41k bits per sub-run cell) cannot resolve the BER.
- Action 17: rate_reduce + power_control (+12 dB link budget), the standard response to attenuation where avoidance does nothing; the expert rule maps path_loss to it and the escalation ladder ends with it.
- Link monitor (`policy_monitor.m`) shared by every policy: 5-frame BER, degradation, class, alarm = threat class, unknown or degradation; confirmed after 2 consecutive alarm cycles. `since` starts saturated (10): no episode clock.
- Shield (`policy_mask.m`, Alshiekh et al., AAAI 2018): without a confirmed alarm the DQN can only keep its configuration or release it to no_action; applied in training (exploration and the Double DQN target maximize over the allowed set) and in deployment. False alarms then follow the detector and the confirmation rule, as for the rule policy. The confirmed flag is a state input (state length 17 + number of configurations).
- Training: learning rate 5e-4 → 5e-5 (exponential), global gradient clipping at 10, per seed the checkpoint with the best greedy return on its own evaluation episodes (train pools). Seed selection: best validation return among seeds whose false-alarm episodes ≤ max(5%, rule + escalation); the gate uses the same limit.
- Baselines: always-on adaptive combining (fixed spatial_diversity), best fixed configuration, expert rule ± escalation, and a class → configuration table tuned on the train pools (`policy_table.m`, the best static mapping the data allows), bandit ablation, one-step oracle. Report: per set, all sets pooled, paired differences DQN − {rule + escalation, table, fixed best, bandit}, restored per threat, restored vs interferer direction.
**Consequences:** phases A5–B3 and C1p–C2e must be rerun (`main.m` flags). The one-shot consumers still wait for the migration to `policy_decide.m` (D44 consequence).

## D46 — Decision layer v3 and phase 4: discount-factor selection, training combinations, one interface, KPIs
**Why:** the D45 run (2026-09-26) was sound but left four points open. (1) γ = 0 beat γ = 0.9 on validation (0.869 vs 0.857) and on the test pools, so γ has to be a hyperparameter chosen on validation, not a fixed 0.9. (2) The agent had never seen any combined threat; on the four test combinations it switched 9.6 times per episode and trailed the best fixed configuration by 0.075. The test combinations stay unseen; what was missing was training coverage of combinations as such. (3) Seven downstream scripts (closed-loop diagnostic, speed, FAR, combined threats, episodes, KPI3, GUI) still used the D39 one-shot interface and could not run against the new agent. (4) The survivability map drew a different random geometry for each action of a cell after D45, so actions were compared on different channels, and it had no clean-reference floor.
**Decision:**
- Training: γ ∈ {0, 0.5, 0.9} × 3 seeds (`CFG.dqn_gammas`, `CFG.dqn_seeds`); the run with the best validation return among the runs within the false-alarm limit is deployed; the best run of every γ is kept for the ablation.
- Training combinations (train split only, 6 geometries): reactive_jamming + path_loss, jamming + antenna_fault, spoofing + sweeping_jammer, benign_interference + noise_burst: every structure of the four test combinations (additive + attenuation, additive + antenna fault, two interferers) with other components. Episodes: 0.75 weight per combination. `build_policy_pools.m` extends an existing pools file (same detector, configurations, Eb/N0 grid and sub-runs) and simulates only the new scenarios; seeds and speeds from `pool_seed.m`.
- False alarms on the clean link count every change (the healthy-link flag of a single test sub-run can be off at high Eb/N0).
- Evaluation (`evaluate_policies.m`): sets single, follower, combined (test combinations), unknown (detector output withheld after onset), clean (768 episodes: FAR with a one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson bound above the detector's Eb/N0 threshold); breakdowns per threat, Eb/N0, interferer direction and UAV speed (speed recovered from the sub-run seed); final configuration per threat.
- One interface: `episode_cycle.m` and `demo_gui.m` run `policy_decide.m` (monitor, confirmation, shield) on detector probabilities and the Mahalanobis score. The live tab passes every frame of the run through the decision layer and applies the configuration at the end of the run; the episode tab simulates every configuration on the same seeds; the unknown-threat slider sets the Mahalanobis retention level (`ood_thresholds.m` keeps the sorted validation scores). The one-shot scripts moved to `legacy/` (table in `legacy/README.md`).
- Latency (`measure_latency.m`): spectrogram, features, CNN, Mahalanobis score, policy; median and p95 with GPU synchronization. `ood_scores.m` computes the isolation-forest score only when asked for.
- KPIs (`measure_all_kpis.m`): the eight KPIs of the proposal from the result files, MET / NOT MET / STALE / MISSING, with explicit targets (restoration ≥ 80% of cycles on recoverable episodes, FAR bound ≤ 5% over ≥ 600 episodes, speed spread ≤ 10 points). `build_kpi_dashboard.m` draws the eight panels from the same files.
- Survivability map: one seed per (entry, level) shared by every action, 57 frames per run, clean reference floored at 1e-4, directional threats mapped at two geometries (interferer 45° and 10° from the GCS direction).
**Consequences:** `main.m` runs B4, OOD, C1p (extension), C2, C2e, SURV, LAT, KPI and DASH in one pass; the detector and the D45 pools are kept.

## D48 — False alarms (alarm definition, M-of-N confirmation on validation), one-pass detector scoring, packet-loss restoration, reward sensitivity
**Why:** KPI 6 failed in D46: 37/768 clean test episodes (4.8%) had a configuration change, one-sided 95% bound 6.29% against 5%. The count was identical for every γ, so it was set by the alarm confirmation, not by the policy; validation had already shown 5.4%. Two sources: (1) the alarm counted the class 'unknown' and 'benign_interference' as evidence of an attack, although the Mahalanobis threshold flags 5% of known frames by construction and benign interference is non-hostile; D33 had already limited unknown gating to a degraded link, and the D45 monitor lost that rule; (2) two consecutive misclassified or noisy frames (2-of-2) confirm too easily on a 30-cycle episode. Latency: the p95 of 48.9 ms came from two separate forward passes of the same network per frame (classification and Mahalanobis embedding).
**Decision:**
- Alarm = a hostile threat class, or link degradation. 'none', 'benign_interference' and 'unknown' raise it only through degradation (D9, D33).
- Confirmation by M-of-N binary integration, the standard trade between detection delay and false-alarm probability in radar detection: confirmed when at least m of the last n cycles carry an alarm. Candidates 2-of-2, 2-of-3, 3-of-3, 3-of-4 are a hyperparameter of training (`CFG.confirm_grid`); each is trained with 3 seeds (γ = 0, chosen in D46) and scored on validation: return on 1280 episodes and false-alarm episodes on 512 clean-link episodes, all on the train pools. Selected: the best return among the runs with ≤ 2% false-alarm episodes (`CFG.fa_val`), a margin for the one-sided bound on the 768 test episodes. The test set is used once, after the choice.
- The rule and the table start a new configuration only on the same confirmed alarm, so every monitored policy is compared under the same confirmation.
- `detect_scores.m`: class probabilities and the Mahalanobis score from one forward pass (outputs 'fc_out' and 'relu_merge'); used by the GUI, `episode_cycle.m` and `measure_latency.m`, which times the detector on GPU and CPU and reports the cycle with the device of lower p95.
- Proposal check: two items of the proposal were not yet measured. Packet loss (KPI "BER and Packet Loss back to at most 2x the no-attack values"): every cycle now also records packet loss back to ≤ clean + 0.05 (the D35 definition, since clean PLR is 0 at high Eb/N0), reported per policy and in KPI 4. Reward-weight sensitivity (mitigation 2, "run again with other weights and check that the conclusions hold"): after the selection the DQN is retrained with every cost term × 0.5 and × 2 and compared with the rule and the table under the same scaled reward on validation.
- Considered: CUSUM on the detector's log-likelihood ratio (quickest change detection) is optimal for a known delay/false-alarm trade but needs calibrated likelihoods for every threat; M-of-N needs no calibration and has one integer pair to choose.
**Consequences:** C2 (with the sensitivity study), C2e, LAT, KPI and DASH rerun; pools and detector unchanged.


## D49 — False alarms, second attempt: alarm definition and false-switch penalty as training hyperparameters; γ reopened; latency class
**Why:** the D48 run (2026-09-26) left KPI 6 unchanged: 37/768 clean test episodes, bound 6.29%. On validation the DQN had 5.1% false-alarm episodes for every confirmation from 2-of-2 to 3-of-4 and for every seed (one exception), while the rule dropped from 42% to 28% with longer confirmation. The D48 premise was therefore wrong: the DQN's false alarms do not come from short noisy alarms but from a fixed set of clean episodes in which the alarm persists for more than four cycles and the agent chooses to act. Two mechanisms can hold such an alarm: a persistent hostile class on a clean link (the detector's residual confusion, e.g. clean at low Eb/N0 against path loss), or a persistent degradation flag. The reward sensitivity pointed at the lever: with every cost ×2 the false alarms fell to 2.1%. The selection fallback also reported the wrong rule when no run met the limit (it took the first run with the fewest false alarms, not the best return among them). The literature check of the proposal sources added two points: the γ = 0 choice was made in D46 under the old 2-of-2 monitor and has to be repeated for the final configuration (with γ = 0 the Double DQN target reduces to the reward), and Oli & Mahalal (IEEE Access 2025, Table 8) classify detection latency as low below 10 ms.
**Decision:**
- Alarm definition becomes a hyperparameter of training (`CFG.alarm_modes`): 'class' (D48: hostile class or degradation) or 'degraded' (degradation only; the class still chooses the response). The reward counts BER, so a threat that does not degrade the link gives nothing to restore; 'degraded' removes alarms from a misclassified but healthy link at the price of a later first reaction.
- False-switch penalty of the training reward (`CFG.fa_penalty_grid` = 20, 40, 80 points per change on a healthy link; standard 20). It shapes training only: validation, the test pools and every baseline use the standard reward, so returns stay comparable. It targets exactly the unwanted action, unlike the ×2 scaling of every cost, which also made real responses more expensive (restored 83% → 74%).
- Grid: 2 alarms × 3 penalties × γ ∈ {0, 0.5, 0.9} × 3 seeds, confirmation 2-of-2 (no effect on the DQN in D48). Selection unchanged (best validation return with ≤ 2% false-alarm episodes); fallback: fewest false alarms, then best return, and the report states which rule applied. The rule and the table run with the selected alarm. Sensitivity study at the selected settings and seed.
- Diagnostics (`rollout_policy.m`, `evaluate_policies.m`): for every episode the cycle, detected class and degradation flag of the first change; the clean-set report lists the DQN and rule false alarms per Eb/N0 and per trigger.
- KPI 7 adds the latency class of Oli & Mahalal, with the note that the time is measured on a desktop computer, not on UAV hardware.
- Comments: stale proposal reference numbers removed from `train_dqn.m`; `fit_ood_model.m` states that the 10% covariance shrinkage is our addition, not part of Lee et al.
**Consequences:** C2 (~2 h), C2e, LAT, KPI and DASH rerun; pools and detector unchanged. If KPI 6 still fails, the trigger diagnostics decide the next step.

## D50 — Attenuation as a change of the link: Eb/N0 drop from the episode's reference
**Why:** the D49 diagnostics located every DQN false alarm on the clean test link: 37 episodes, all at Eb/N0 = 2 dB, all with the detector reading path_loss, none with the link degraded (about one of the four test geometries of that cell). The count was the same for every seed, penalty and γ = 0 / 0.5: the agent sees exactly the state of a real path-loss episode, where rate_reduce + power_control is the right answer, so no reward shaping can separate them. Physically they are the same signal: path loss is a 10 dB attenuation, and an attenuated link at 10 dB receives what a clean link receives at 0–2 dB. The frame alone cannot tell them apart; the link's history can. The 'degraded' alarm of D49 confirmed the mechanism from the other side: the monitor measures degradation against the clean BER at the receiver's own Eb/N0 estimate, and path loss lowers that estimate with the BER, so path loss never counts as degradation; the 'degraded' runs lost 0.07 of return (restored 68% vs 83%). D49 also showed γ = 0.9 with 0 false alarms on the test clean set but 5–17% on validation, so it could not be selected, and that the false-switch penalty lowers the validation false alarms only slightly (5.1% → 4.1% at γ = 0).
**Decision:**
- `policy_monitor.m` keeps, per episode, the Eb/N0 estimate (signal over thermal noise, SINR + IoT, insensitive to added interference) and a reference: the median of the last 10 cycles without an alarm, or the current value before any such cycle. Drop = reference − median of the last 3 cycles.
- The drop is a new DQN state input (`policy_state.m`, state length 18 + number of configurations).
- Alarm mode 'class_drop': as 'class', but the class path_loss raises the alarm only after a drop of at least 4 dB (`PP.drop_db`, 40% of the modeled attenuation, above the 3-frame fading spread of the Rician K = 10 two-antenna link). Grid: 'class' / 'class_drop' × false-switch penalty 20 / 80 × γ 0 / 0.5 / 0.9 × 3 seeds; selection unchanged.
- Diagnostics add the drop at the first change of every episode.
- Limitation: attenuation that is present from the first frame of a run has no reference and is not recognized as path loss under 'class_drop' (it still acts through degradation, if any). Episodes in training and evaluation, the survivability map and the episode tab of the GUI all start clean; the live tab of the GUI starts with the threat.
**Consequences:** new state length: C2 retrains, C2e, LAT, KPI and DASH rerun; pools and detector unchanged. 'degraded' stays in `policy_monitor.m` as a documented negative result.

## D51 — Decision layer frozen; KPI 6 measured on independent geometries
**Why:** the D50 run (2026-09-27) left the DQN false alarms at 37/768 test episodes, again all at 2 dB, all read as path_loss, not degraded, and with a median Eb/N0 drop of 4.2 dB: in that geometry the clean signal really fades by about as much as an attenuation attack, so the history does not separate them either. Three design rounds (D48–D50) have now addressed the false alarms; the remaining cases are a physical ambiguity, and further tuning would be driven by diagnostics taken on the test set. The measurement itself is also weak: the test pools hold 4 geometries per Eb/N0, so the 768 clean episodes come from 24 channels, the episodes of one channel are strongly correlated (they cycle through the same 20 frames), and all 37 false alarms come from one of them. The one-sided Clopper–Pearson bound over 768 episodes assumes independent trials and overstates the certainty; at the level of geometries, 1 of 24 has an upper bound near 19%.
**Decision:**
- The decision layer is frozen at the D50 selection (alarm 'class', false-switch penalty 80, γ = 0.5, seed 44). No further change is motivated by test-set results.
- `build_clean_test_pools.m` measures the clean link on 100 new geometries per Eb/N0 (seed block 3 of `pool_seed.m`, never used before), under all 17 configurations with common random numbers, with the same detector, 20 frames per geometry and the same features: 10,200 simulations, `data/clean_test_pools.mat`.
- `evaluate_policies.m` runs one clean episode per new geometry (600 independent episodes, `link_env` accepts the sub-run of each episode in the spec) for every policy; KPI 6 (≥ 600 episodes, one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson bound) is computed on this set. The 4-geometry clean set stays in the report for comparison. The false-alarm report became one local function (`far_report`) used for both sets.
- The KPI 6 target stays 5% until the supervisor decides; the result is reported in full (episode rate, bound, per-cycle rate, triggers, return and goodput on the clean link) whichever side of the target it falls on.
**Consequences:** C1c (~70 min), C2e, KPI and DASH run; no training.

## D52 — Path-loss alarm threshold chosen from data; alarm selected on an independent clean validation set
**Why:** the D51 measurement (23/600 independent clean geometries, bound 5.39%) and the speed diagnostic of 2026-09-27 (`diagnostics/diag_speed.m`) located the false alarms: the detector reads about 4.5% of clean frames as a threat at every UAV speed, almost all path_loss at Eb/N0 ≤ 4 dB, and the confusion is symmetric (15 of 20 clean-frame errors are path_loss, 19 of 22 path_loss errors are 'none'). The fade-duration hypothesis (short decisions mistake long fades) was rejected: the error rate does not grow at low speed, where fades last 2.5× longer. Two facts from the code explain the rest. (1) An episode walks through the 20 recorded frames of its geometry in order (`link_env.m`), so a geometry with two consecutive misread frames alarms in every episode, whatever the policy learned: the 2.1% floor of every validation run is one such geometry among 36, and a longer confirmation cannot remove a run of misreads that the detector's 10-frame feature window makes persistent. (2) The D50 gate on the Eb/N0 drop was the right information with a guessed threshold: 4 dB is the size of the fades that mislead the detector (median 4.2 dB at the first change), while the modeled attenuation is 10 dB, so the gate let the fades through.
**Decision:**
- `choose_drop_threshold.m` (C1d) chooses the threshold on the train split only: the drop of every path_loss frame against the drop of every clean frame the detector reads as path_loss, both measured as `policy_monitor.m` measures them (3-frame median against the clean reference of the same geometry). Threshold = midpoint between the 95th percentile of the misread clean frames and the 5th percentile of the path_loss frames when they separate; otherwise the largest margin, flagged. Saved in `data/drop_threshold.mat` and carried in `trained_dqn.mat` to every consumer of the alarm.
- `build_clean_test_pools.m` also builds an independent clean validation set (seed block 4, 100 geometries per Eb/N0, `data/clean_val_pools.mat`), so the alarm is selected on geometries that are neither the 6 train geometries nor the 600 test geometries.
- `train_dqn.m`: every run and both baselines are also scored on one clean episode per validation geometry. Selection rule, fixed before the run: the best-return run with alarm 'class' is the baseline; 'class_drop' replaces it only with fewer false-alarm episodes on the independent set (Fisher exact test, p < 0.05) and a validation return within 0.01. Grid reduced to the D50 selection (penalty 80, γ = 0.5, 3 seeds) × the two alarms. The test set is measured once, after the selection.
- Rejected for now: a longer confirmation for path_loss only (D48 showed 2-of-2 to 3-of-4 leave the false alarms unchanged, and the misreads persist over the feature window); hard negatives for the detector (the same frame is physically ambiguous; a new detector would rebuild the pools, about 6 h); a longer decision cycle (needs longer recordings per geometry; rejected by the speed diagnostic anyway).
- Reporting: the per-episode rate is a property of the 20 recorded frames of a geometry; the per-cycle rate is also reported as configuration changes per second of flight (0.19% × 1,940 cycles/s ≈ 3.7 per second on the clean link in D51), and the 20-frame recording per geometry as a limitation of the pools.
**Consequences:** C1c (validation set, 66 min), C1d, C2 (6 runs), C2e, LAT, KPI and DASH ran on 2026-09-27: threshold 6.5 dB, KPI 6 met (9/600, bound 2.60%), 8/8 KPIs. Limitations of D50 stand: attenuation present from the first frame has no reference; a gradual attenuation would pass under the threshold. antenna_fault reads (4 of 23) and the spoofing read are not gated.

## D56 — 3D episode view

## D59 — v4: receiver-side measurements, spatial awareness, combined configurations, stricter evaluation
**Why:** a full review of the project against the approved proposal, the updated proposal, the lecturer's proposal and every cited source (docs/LITERATURE.md, 2026-09-28) found that most detector features and the decision layer's link monitor used information a receiver does not have (the BER counted against the transmitted bits, a packet counted lost at BER > 10%, SINR and envelope correlation fitted against the transmitted waveform); that the detector saw one antenna and the agent had no information on the interferer's direction, although the central finding of the project is geometric (D45, D46); that even the oracle restored only 9-54% of the combined threats because no configuration combined avoidance, nulling and link budget; that an episode's frames before and after the onset came from two different flights (the pool seed depended on the scenario); that the decision layer was tested at nominal severity only, on 4 test geometries, validated on its own training geometries, with t-intervals over episodes that share geometries (the proposal says bootstrap by runs); and that the packet-loss criterion and the 'no response' baseline did not match the proposal. The user set a target of >= 90% for detection, response to every threat including combined ones, and decision speed, and asked that every change rest on the literature of the proposal documents.
**Decision:**
- *Receiver measurements* (`extract_closed_loop_frames.m`): packet loss from a CRC-32 check of every frame (the actual error pattern applied to a CRC-protected codeword; the 32 CRC bits were already reserved in the frame); BER estimated from the combiner output (decision-directed SNR per 32-symbol block, BER = mean of Q(sqrt(SNR)), Simon & Alouini); SINR and envelope correlation against the waveform re-modulated from the receiver's own decisions; spatial coherence of the interference, predicted MMSE gain over MRC and alignment of the dominant interference direction with the GCS channel, from the channel estimator the MMSE combiner already runs (Richards, ch. 9; the known-symbol estimator is the assumption declared in proposal risk 8). The true BER and frame error stay, for the KPIs and the training reward only. Detector input: 13 link features (`link_features.m`).
- *Dataset*: 8 sub-runs per cell instead of 5 (train 5, validation 1, test 2).
- *Unknown threats*: Mahalanobis feature ensemble over five layers with logistic weights fitted on known validation frames against their FGSM versions, as in Lee et al. (no unknown-threat sample used); the last-layer score is kept for comparison.
- *Architecture comparison* (approved proposal, risk 5 mitigation): hybrid vs spectrogram-only vs link-features-only on the same splits (`compare_architectures.m`), as in the multimodal comparison of Tariq et al.
- *Configurations* (`policy_actions.m`): one choice per domain — frequency (none, channel_switch, freq_diversity) x space (none, spatial_diversity) x link budget (none, rate, power, FEC, rate+power, power+FEC) = 36, the combined action of Liu et al.
- *Pools* (`build_policy_pools.m`, `pool_cell.m`): single threats at three severities (`decision_config.m`), train 6 / validation 4 / test 8 geometries per (cell, Eb/N0); the same geometries for every cell, so an episode stays in one flight; identical physics simulated once; parallel workers (`pool_worker_init.m`).
- *Agent*: state = the last 4 cycles of receiver observations (Liu et al.'s waterfall, Mnih et al.'s stacked frames) + configuration, dwell, confirmed alarm; reward 100 when the link is restored (BER <= 2x clean), otherwise at most 40 (log-linear; below 100 minus the largest running cost, so restoring always pays), minus costs — aligned with KPI 4 as in Liu et al.'s rate-if-successful reward; checkpoints and selection on the validation split by recovered episodes, under the false-alarm bound; hidden layers 256-256, 20000 episodes.
- *Monitor and rules*: degradation from the estimated BER against the clean link's estimated BER (like with like), or from the CRC packet loss when FEC is on; the rule also nulls separable interferers (predicted MMSE gain >= 6 dB) and escalates through combined configurations.
- *Evaluation*: 'no response' baseline; KPI 4 = recovered episodes (BER and packet loss <= 2x clean for 5 consecutive cycles) among the recoverable ones, per threat and severity; packet loss <= 2x clean + one packet; 95% intervals by bootstrap over geometries (`boot_cluster.m`).
- *Survivability*: the map runs on the 36 configurations and requires both BER and packet loss; `experiment_survivability_options.m` repeats recoverability for the combined threats and the directional threats at high severity with 2 antennas, 3 antennas (an N-element array nulls N-1 interferers, Richards) and 2 antennas plus a relay path (the signal arriving 60 deg off the GCS direction with 3 dB extra loss; Papathanasiou et al.'s secondary communication paths, the lecturer's alternate route). Both are experiments of deliverable 8; the system itself keeps two antennas and one path.
- *Tooling*: `run_stage.m` (logged stages, smoke mode), `decision_config.m` (constants in one place), git commit in the KPI report.
- Dropped for lack of a basis in the proposal literature: CUSUM change detection, finer power and rate levels, a recurrent Q-network.
**Consequences:** everything from the dataset on is regenerated and every result is new; v3 data and results are kept in `data_backup_v3_20260928` and `results_backup_v3_20260928`. The updated proposal (new file next to the original) states the new KPI targets: every detection class >= 90%, unknown-threat AUROC >= 0.9, >= 90% of the recoverable episodes of every threat restored (three severities, combined threats included), decision latency median < 10 ms and p95 < 20 ms.

## D60 — unknown-threat score: the last layer, chosen by nested leave-one-threat-out selection
**Why:** in the first v4 leave-one-threat-out run (43,200-frame dataset, receiver measurements only) the Mahalanobis feature ensemble of D59 reached a mean AUROC of 0.803, below the last layer alone (0.863) on 7 of 8 held-out threats; its logistic weights, fitted on known validation frames against their FGSM versions, gave the first layer a negative weight (-1.19): they learn what separates adversarial noise, not a new threat. The complete method of Lee et al. also has input pre-processing, which was not implemented in D59.
**Decision:**
- `diagnostics/diag_ood_scores.m` tests on the same folds every layer alone, the ensemble, the last layer, input pre-processing with the noise magnitude chosen on validation (Lee et al.'s list; logistic fit on half of the validation sub-runs, AUROC known vs FGSM on the other half), MSP and energy, and a nested selection: for each held-out threat, the score with the best mean AUROC on the other seven. The held-out threat never fits, weights or chooses anything.
- Validation chose no pre-processing (eps = 0) for all 8 folds and the ensemble; pre-processing also costs 84-105 ms per frame, too slow for a decision cycle. The nested selection picked the last layer for all 8 folds (mean AUROC 0.867).
- The production unknown-threat score is the Mahalanobis distance of the last hidden layer ('relu_merge', `M.score = 'last'` in `fit_ood_model.m`), as in the proposal (Mahalanobis on the network's internal features [6]); the ensemble is still fitted and reported for comparison. `detect_scores.m` asks the network for the logits and that layer only.
- `eval_ood_detection.m` reports the nested selection and the score averaged over the frames of the alarm confirmation (2) and of the monitor window (5) as well as the per-frame AUROC, which stays the KPI value.
**Consequences:** the weak threat is reactive jamming (AUROC ~0.64-0.70): from the receiver it is a jammer that is on whenever our transmitter is, which on a continuous uplink looks like barrage jamming; 85% of its frames are read as jamming, which calls for the same countermeasure. Averaging over 5 frames adds ~0.02 (frames of one run share the channel, so their scores are correlated). The per-frame mean AUROC (~0.86-0.87) is above the 0.8 of the submitted proposal and below the 0.9 of the updated proposal (D59).

## D61 — v4 completed: per-antenna feature, unknown-threat score chosen by leave-one-threat-out, learning a new threat, combined threats in training, monitor grid, decision period, code in code/
**Why:** the reduced v4 runs of 2026-09-28 (one monitor, penalty 80, γ 0.5, one seed) left four open points. (1) False alarms: the DQN changed configuration in 27 of 600 independent clean episodes (4.5%, one-sided bound 6.15% > 5%); the misreads were antenna_fault and path_loss at low Eb/N0, when a fade hits the one antenna the detector looked at. (2) Combined threats: with 4 combinations in training and 4 others only in test, sweeping_jammer+path_loss was recovered in 5.2% of recoverable episodes (oracle 97.9%) and KPI 4 held for 9 of 12 threats; the proposal asks for every threat including the combined ones. (3) Unknown threats: D60 fixed the last layer after one diagnostic; the choice among scores should itself be made by the evaluation, with an honest estimate. (4) One decision cycle was one frame (0.516 ms), so times in cycles did not map to the 20 ms latency target. The user decided: KPI 2 target 0.8 as in the submitted proposal (the approved form gives no number; the 0.9 of D59 is withdrawn); combined threats in training (option A) plus a separate study of combinations never trained on; Lee et al.'s Algorithm 2 added; two antennas kept, three reported as an experiment; m-of-n confirmation chosen on validation; a 20 ms decision period with the time-scale limitation documented.
**Decision:**
- *Per-antenna feature* `branch_dip` (`extract_closed_loop_frames.m`, `link_features.m`, 14 link features): for each UAV antenna, the median minus the minimum of the per-32-symbol channel gain in dB over the frame, the larger of the two antennas (frame 1 skips its first block). A clean link stays below 6 dB, an antenna fault is above 10 dB (unit test). A tested alternative, the gain ratio between branches, did not separate the misreads and was dropped.
- *Unknown-threat score* (`ood_score_set.m`, `fit_ood_model.m`, `eval_ood_detection.m`): candidates last layer, Lee et al.'s ensemble, Mahalanobis on the link features, and the lower of two validation-standardized scores (last layer or link features; last layer or isolation forest). Leave-one-threat-out chooses the production score by mean AUROC and writes it into the detector file before the pools are built; the KPI 2 value is the nested estimate (for each held-out threat, the score chosen on the other seven). Isolation forest as in Liu et al. (100 trees, subsample min(8192, N), trained on known frames only).
- *Learning a new threat* (Lee et al., Algorithm 2): once flagged and labelled, the held-out threat becomes a new class from K of its validation frames (class mean, tied covariance updated as C/(C+1) Σ + 1/(C+1) Σ_new) without retraining, classified by the smallest Mahalanobis distance. Reduced run: K = 100 frames give 87.9% recall of the new threat and known-class accuracy 96.0% → 94.8% (K = 20: 58.8%).
- *Combined threats* (`build_policy_pools.m`, `dqn_episode_spec.m`): all 8 combinations in the train, validation and test pools, test on flight geometries never used in training (KPI 4); training draws each combination with weight 2. `experiment_combo_generalization.m` (stage C2g) retrains the DQN without one combination at a time and tests it on that combination, next to the DQN that trained on it, rule + escalation and the table.
- *Monitor* (`train_dqn.m`): the alarm 'class_drop' with confirmation 2 of 2 or 3 of 3 cycles (M-of-N, Richards), chosen on validation with the false-switch penalty, γ and the seed under the false-alarm bound; the alarm 'class' alone gave 20-37% false alarms and is not in the grid.
- *Decision period* (`decision_config.m`): 20 ms, the p95 latency target. Recovery times are reported in cycles and in ms. The pools hold consecutive frames, so fading between two cycles is more correlated than 20 ms apart (limitation). Pools with 20 ms spacing were rejected: about 20× the simulation time.
- *DQN inference* (`dqn_dense.m`, `policy_decide.m`): the trained network is stored as its weight matrices and evaluated by matrix products, which removes the framework overhead from the decision cycle.
- *Shield, stated formally* (`policy_mask.m`, Alshiekh et al.): the safety property is G(¬confirmed_t → a_t ∈ {a_{t-1}, no_action}): without a confirmed alarm a policy may only keep its configuration or release it. The shield is preemptive (it removes the other configurations before the agent chooses, in training and in deployment, and the Double DQN target maximizes over the allowed set); escalation ranks the allowed configurations and acts only after 3 degraded cycles, which confirm the alarm under both 2/2 and 3/3, so it keeps the property.
- *Antennas*: the system keeps two UAV antennas (the approved design, D41; one null, Richards: N-1 interferers). `experiment_survivability_options.m` (SURV3) reports three antennas and a relay path; `n_rx` stays a parameter.
- *Detector at unseen severities* (`eval_unseen_severity.m`, stage B4s): as B4 does for Eb/N0, the production detector classifies fresh frames at the training levels, between them and above them up to the map's levels (22 and 28 dB JSR, path loss 26 dB); rule fixed before the run: the training levels are extended when, above the range, fewer than 90% of a threat's frames are read as a class with the same countermeasure.
- *Comb jammer* (`evaluate_policies.m`, set 'comb'): Liu et al. list four jammers (sweep, comb, random, intelligent); sweep, random (time-partial) and intelligent (the follower) were covered, comb (every usable channel jammed at once) was not. From the receiver a comb jammer is jamming on our channel that is already there when we hop, i.e. the follower with zero re-acquisition delay, so it needs no new class or data: a separate test set of jamming and reactive jamming with immediate re-acquisition, reported apart from KPI 4. Protocol attacks listed by the surveys (replay, injection, man-in-the-middle, eavesdropping, DDoS) and GNSS attacks are outside the physical-layer scope of the proposal.
- *Survivability map*: in-band threat levels up to 28 dB JSR (Liu et al. evaluate a 30 dBm jammer against a 0 dBm signal); path loss up to 26 dB.
- *Disk guard* (`disk_guard.m`): every simulation leaves its logged data in the Simulation Data Inspector repository (a .dmr file per MATLAB process); killed parallel workers left 238 GB. The repository is emptied after every simulation, and the pipeline stops when the drive has less than 200 GB free or the .dmr files exceed 2 GB.
- *Layout*: all code in `code/` (`link`, `detection`, `decision`, `evaluation`, `gui`, `tests`, `diagnostics`, `legacy`); `startup.m` at the root puts it on the path (D47).
- *Code comments*: a file explains what it does now, briefly per section; the history of a change is kept in these documents only. Applied to every file touched from now on; a pass over all files at the end of the project.
**Consequences:** the dataset gains a feature, so the full chain reruns from A0 (about 16 h, stages A0-DASH plus C2g and SURV3). Until then the results in the README are the D52 (v3) results, and the files in `results/` on the branch are those of D52.

## D62 — Path-loss alarm threshold chosen on validation; DQN selected by its weakest threat
**Why:** the first test reading of the v4 decision layer (2026-09-29 08:50, kept in `archive/v4_first_test_20260929/`) met KPI 5 (+15.7 points over rule + escalation) and KPI 6 (14/600 clean episodes, bound 3.62%), but KPI 4 held for 14 of 16 threats: path loss 80.2% (low severity 66.7%) and reactive jamming + path loss 89.6%. A diagnosis on the validation split only (`diagnostics/diag_path_loss.m`) located the cause: the monitor compares the estimated BER with the clean link's at the receiver's own Eb/N0 estimate, so a pure attenuation looks like a clean link at lower Eb/N0 and only the path_loss class with an Eb/N0 drop of at least the threshold raises the alarm. At 6 dB of attenuation the noisy drop estimate often stays below the 5.0 dB chosen on the train pools (`choose_drop_threshold.m`, largest margin between path-loss frames kept and misread clean frames passed); 36.7% of the recoverable low-severity episodes never got a confirmed alarm (77% at 0 dB). With the same agent, 4.0 dB raised low-severity recovery to 85.2% (false alarms 22/600 on the independent clean validation geometries) and 3.0 dB to 92.2% (27/600). The train-pool rule sees only frames; the trade-off it sets belongs to the episodes, as the m-of-n confirmation does.
**Decision:**
- *Threshold in the validation grid* (`train_dqn.m`): the train-pool threshold and 1 and 2 dB below it (on the train pools they keep 88%, 95% and 99% of path-loss frames), each trained with every monitor, discount factor and seed. The false-switch penalty 20 is dropped from the grid: it exceeded the false-alarm bound on validation in all 12 of its runs.
- *Validation on every cell*: each run is evaluated on every (threat cell, Eb/N0, geometry) of the validation split twice, instead of a random mix, so a per-threat recovery exists for every threat.
- *Selection by the KPI 4 statistic*: among runs within the false-alarm bound (KPI 6), the highest recovery of the weakest threat (severities pooled, as KPI 4 is worded); within one point, the best pooled recovery, then the return.
**Consequences:** C2, C2e, C2g, LAT, KPI and DASH rerun. The test set is read a second time; the first reading is reported next to the second, as in D52. The change was chosen on validation data only.

## D63 — Hold in the shield for every policy; clean-link false alarms per monitor; oracle under an immediate follower
**Why:**
- *Ping-pong.* The rule and table policies commit a change only after a dwell and never within `C.hold` = 3 cycles of the previous one (proposal mitigation 8, decision hysteresis; D31 measured it: DQN switches per episode 18.5 → 1.5). Since D45 the DQN had only the switching cost in its reward. `diagnostics/diag_switching.m` on the validation split (agent of the first test reading, every cell × Eb/N0 × geometry twice, 1,584 episodes): 4.17 changes per episode against 2.84 for rule + escalation; 58.9% of the DQN's changes came within 3 cycles of the previous one and 20.8% of the episodes went A → B → A within 3 cycles (noise burst + antenna fault 72.9%, spoofing + noise burst 47.9%, noise burst 38.2%); the rule: 0% and 0% by construction. A configuration change is a coordinated reconfiguration of both ends of the link; one every 20 ms back and forth is not an operating mode.
- *False alarms per monitor.* `clean_world` in `train_dqn.m` cached the whole PP of its first call, including the monitor (alarm, m-of-n, drop threshold), so since D59 every run's false-alarm count on the independent clean validation geometries used the first block's monitor: rule + escalation read 38.7% for every monitor and threshold. With the monitor of each call, rule + escalation: 2/2 at 4 dB 237/600, 2/2 at 3 dB 240/600, 3/3 at 4 dB 160/600. The 2/2 runs of every earlier grid were scored correctly; the 3/3 runs were scored with 2/2. The first reading's selection (2/2) stands: its runs were scored correctly and none of the 3/3 runs had a higher recovery. The D62 removal of penalty 20 stands on the 2/2 runs alone (64–229/600 against a bound near 21/600) and on its recovery, no better than penalty 80.
- *Oracle.* The one-step oracle assumed that a channel hop always escapes the follower for the next frame. With `fdelay` 0 (the comb set, Liu et al.) the environment re-acquires the new channel in the same frame; the oracle hopped every cycle and recovered 0.7% of the comb episodes in the first test reading.
**Decision:**
- *Hold in the shield* (`policy_mask.m`): within `C.hold` cycles of the last change only the current configuration is allowed, for the DQN as for the rule and table, in training (action choice and the Double DQN target) and in deployment (`policy_decide.m`). One constant for every policy; the agent's state already holds the cycles since its last change.
- *Monitor per call* in `clean_world`: only the pools and their tables are cached.
- *Oracle*: a hop escapes for `fdelay` frames, none when `fdelay` is 0.
- *Grid*: the drop threshold 1 and 2 dB below the train-pool value. On the 29 D62 runs that finished before the grid was stopped, the train-pool value (5 dB) left path loss as the weakest threat in 11 of 12 runs (70–75%); at 4 and 3 dB the weakest threat was at 69–85% and path loss was the weakest in 2 of 17. All other grid axes unchanged: 2 monitors × 2 thresholds × 2 discount factors × 3 seeds.
**Consequences:** C2, C2e, C2g, GAL, LAT, KPI and DASH rerun. The D62 grid was stopped at 29 of 36 runs before any of its agents was evaluated on the test set, so the test set is read a second time with the D63 agent; the first reading stays next to it. The hold and the thresholds were decided on validation data and the proposal; the other two are corrections of the code (the oracle is a reference row, not the evaluated system).

## D64 — Escalation out of no_action on a persistent confirmed alarm; DQN + escalation deployed; followers of 0–5 cycles in training; new test flights
**Why:** validation-split diagnostics of the D63 agent (`diagnostics/diag_failure_modes.m`, `diag_never_acted.m`; every cell × Eb/N0 × geometry twice).
- *Deployed policy.* DQN and DQN + escalation are equal on static threats (95.1% / 95.1% of the recoverable episodes); with a follower that re-acquires the channel 2–5 cycles after a hop 81.8% / 87.2%; with an immediate follower (0 cycles) 63.7% / 85.0%. 93–95% of the DQN's follower failures held a channel_switch configuration that restores the link only while the hop escapes. Releases to no_action while the threat was on: 0.3–0.4% of the episodes.
- *Antenna fault.* All static antenna-fault failures were episodes with no configuration change after onset. In every such episode (44 of 144 recoverable) the alarm was confirmed (on in 72–96% of the cycles, detector reading antenna_fault in 71–95%), while the monitor's degradation flag was on in 3–8%: the fault is intermittent (duty 0.08–0.3), and the 5-frame window of the estimated BER stays near clean while the geometry's mean BER exceeds 2× clean. 29–50% of the antenna-fault geometries need no reconfiguration at all, so with the training false-switch penalty (80) waiting is the better bet when the link looks clean, and escalation, triggered by degraded cycles, never fired. Path loss: 5 such episodes, none with a confirmed alarm (Eb/N0 drop 0.3–0.4 dB), a monitor miss left as it is.
- *False alarms of escalation.* On the 600 independent clean validation geometries: DQN 17, DQN + escalation 23 (one-sided bound 5.39% > 5%). The 6 extra episodes escalated out of no_action after 3 degraded cycles with the class reading unknown or benign interference.
- *Monitor against the KPI.* The monitor's 'degraded' flag was on in 8–52% of the cycles in which the applied configuration truly restored BER and packet loss (power_control 40.5%, rate_reduce + power_control 51.8%), and 61–90% of the escalations left a restoring configuration; escalation still recovered 52 episodes the DQN lost, against 15 the other way. A reference at the pre-incident Eb/N0 estimate (the KPI's own reference) cut the false flags (power_control 20.5%) but lowered the follower threats (jamming and reactive jamming −3.5 points each) and was not adopted (rule set before the run: no threat lower by more than 2 episodes). A 5-frame window cannot resolve a 2× BER ratio near the pools' BER floor: a stated limitation.
**Decision:**
- *Escalation* (`policy_decide.m`, every *_esc policy): out of a configuration the policy applied, as before (degraded for `C.esc` cycles); out of no_action, only when the alarm has stayed confirmed for `C.esc` cycles (`mem.conf_n`, `policy_monitor.m`). Validation, D63 agent: pooled 93.3% → 94.1%, antenna fault 91.7% → 100%, clean false alarms 23 → 18 of 600 (bound 4.42%), no threat lower. The count is the existing constant `C.esc` = 3; 5 gave the same.
- *Deployed policy: DQN + escalation.* KPIs, evaluation, dashboard, latency and the operator console use it; `train_dqn.m` scores checkpoints, runs, clean false alarms and the reward sensitivity with it.

## D68 — v5: the full threat envelope, two further threats, measured channel ranges, signalling delay

## D72 — v6: evidence over time, a quiet slot, detector levels up to the sources' caps
**Why (2026-10-01, with the user, after the v5 detector reading):** the user asked for solutions, not limits, for the weak detector classes. The v5 reading traced them to three causes:
- *One frame of 0.5 ms decides.* The fading does not change inside it (coherence 1.2-6.6 ms at 29-161 km/h), so a deep fade on one antenna reads as a hidden antenna (clean link F1 84.6%, airframe shadowing 85.9%, weak shadowing 5-7 dB at 16-64%). In the pools the decision cycles were consecutive frames, 0.5 ms apart (frame-to-frame gain correlation 0.82), which also explains why the CNN-LSTM of D13 gained nothing from the sequence.
- *Two threat pairs were the same physics while we transmit.* A reactive jammer is on whenever our continuous link is on; benign interference is continuous noise like jamming, only weaker (jamming F1 88.5%; reactive jamming learned as a new class 50.6%, 54.9% even from 960 frames).
- *The unknown-threat score read "stronger than trained" as "new":* a 30 dB tone, correctly classified, was flagged unknown in 57.5% of its frames, and a frame flagged unknown loses its class in the monitor.
**Decision (v6, branch v6-dev):**
- *Channel clock of one decision cycle per frame* (`cycle_s` 20 ms): the sum-of-sinusoids fading (ours and the interferers') is evaluated at the cycle times, so consecutive frames are one cycle apart as the decisions are (measured frame-to-frame gain correlation -0.25..0.10 vs 0.82). No extra simulation cost.
- *Quiet slot* (`quiet_symbols` 32, 5.8% of air time): the GCS is silent for the last 32 symbols of every frame (the gap between command packets). The UAV measures the channel without our signal: q_iot (interference over thermal in the slot) and q_react (residual while we transmit over the slot). Jammer detection on unused/empty pilots (Pirayesh & Zeng, 2022 survey); a reactive jammer transmits only while it senses our signal (Xu et al.; Sagduyu et al.). Measured at Eb/N0 9 dB: jamming q_iot 28.3 / q_react -0.2 dB, reactive jamming 0.0 / 24.5 dB, tone 22.3 / -2.0, spoofing 9.9 / -1.4, benign 7.1 / 0.0, clean 0.0 / ~0. The slot is longer than the filter span, so no data symbol straddles two frames (the channel moves on inside it) and the receiver outputs each frame's own bits (delay 0). BER without fading equals v5 and theory (0 dB: 7.6e-3 / 7.7e-3 / 7.2e-3).
- *Temporal fusion* (`temporal_evidence.m`, `fuse_classes.m`, stage B2F `select_fusion.m`): the class of a cycle from the last N cycles: the mean log-probability of the per-frame detector, the gap between the antennas' local-mean gains (Lee: local average over 20-40 wavelengths; shadowing lasts seconds, Khawaja et al.), the share of cycles with the same weakest antenna, and the share with quiet-slot interference; a multinomial logistic regression fitted on the validation split, N and the regularization by 5-fold cross-validation over validation sub-runs. The monitor uses it for the class; the agent's state gets the fused probabilities, q_iot, q_react and the three persistence measurements. KPI 1 is read on the decisions of the system (fused), with the per-frame reading beside it.
- *Unknown-threat score over a window:* the score and its averaging window (1, 2, 5, 8 cycles) are chosen together by the leave-one-threat-out procedure, nested estimate quoted.
- *Detector levels up to the sources' most severe values:* in-band threats 0-30 dB (Liu et al.), airframe shadowing 5-35 dB (Khawaja et al.); the unseen-severity test keeps the levels between the training levels and above them only where the sources go further.
- *Severity ranges of the sources* (the user's rule: the most severe value measured, never beyond; detector and decision layer): in-band interferers 30 dB (Liu et al.); airframe shadowing 40 dB (Sun, NASA measurements: up to 40 dB, events of about 30 s; aircraft antenna diversity mitigates it, GS diversity does not); path loss 22 dB (Cui et al. 2019: building blockage adds 21.9 dB, maximum shadow fading 22.4 dB at 4 GHz); spoofer 30 dB, the jammer's hardware (a 0.2-3 dB advantage captures the receiver, Whitehouse et al.; the hijacker uses the jammer's radio, Mekdad et al.).
- *Antenna fault as a connector opening at an airframe vibration frequency* drawn per flight in 86-672 Hz (motor and frame modes of a multirotor, Verbeke & Debruyne), open for the duty cycle, 30 dB while open (an open contact couples only through its gap capacitance: 0.01-0.03 pF at 2.4 GHz in 50 ohm gives 26-36 dB). The previous 5 kHz toggling was faster than any mechanical cause.
- *Benign interference as WLAN packet traffic* (user approval): packets of 0.27-3.2 ms (Wollenberg et al.), channel occupancy drawn per flight in 5-86% (Cheema & Salous; Wollenberg et al.), exponential idle gaps, on the channel clock; power up to 30 dB over our signal (EIRP 20 dBm, ETSI EN 300 328, against the link budget; interference grows with altitude, Song et al.). The threat block reports the share of each frame with a packet on the air; dataset frames of a benign sub-run without a packet are labelled clean.
- *PHY validation at the envelope's worst-case Doppler (358 Hz) instead of 1000 Hz:* the 1000 Hz served only to collect independent fades quickly; with the channel clock every frame is an independent fade at any Doppler. At 1000 Hz the channel changes inside the 64-symbol estimation window (BER / theory 1.16, 1.40, 2.24 at 2, 6, 10 dB, K = -5 dB, three antennas), which the perfect-CSI theory does not include; at 358 Hz 1.05, 1.13, 1.31. The receiver's estimation windows now run over the frame's data symbols, the last one taking the remainder (no short window next to the quiet slot).

## D73 — v7: a real receiver, flight dynamics and source-corrected severities
**Why (2026-10-01, with the user):** a review of the whole source library (docs/v7_source_review.md, docs/v7_research_notes/) found that the method rests on sources that hold up, but several severity caps and shapes went beyond what the cited sources measured; the ideal receiver (known timing, frequency and symbols) hid how a real receiver fails; hover and turns were missing from the flight envelope. The user's rules: everything RF and only RF (detect, monitor and overcome threats; flight conditions only through their RF effect); where no source backs a value, lower it to what a source backs and state the rest as the system's limit; a good system states its envelope (speed, range, threats).
**Decision:**
- *Frame and receiver.* Quiet slot before the frame (interference measured just before the frame is found) and a guard after it; 802.11-style short and long training (Pirayesh & Zeng) and a pilot block of 4 symbols after every 48 data symbols (802.11: 4 of 52); each radio within ±25 ppm (IEEE 802.11-2007, 18.4.7.4/18.4.7.5), an unknown fractional arrival per frame and the LoS Doppler shift on the air. Synchronization in a whitened domain chosen per frame among the quiet slot, the training region (power inversion, Ogawa et al.) and segment-local power inversion; timing and coarse frequency found jointly on the whole training by an FFT grid search (Morelli & D'Amico; La Pan et al.); residual frequency from the pilot phase progression; first MMSE pass by local whitening; four decision-directed passes weighing the leave-one-out channel with the covariance of the received samples (MVDR), so a wrong decision cannot pull our own direction into it.
- *What the real receiver shows (validate_phy V11, 161 km/h):* 1.0 dB from the ideal receiver on AWGN, 0.8 dB with three antennas; under a 10 dB jammer an error floor the ideal receiver does not have (BER 2e-3 at 10 dB after the MVDR passes, 6.6 dB in the validation before them); against a 30 dB jammer whose channel fades at 222-358 Hz three antennas cannot hold the null (covariance aging: Truong & Heath; Winters-type DMI, US 6,147,985; Shepard et al., Argos), so the decision layer has to answer it (corrected in D75: about half of that loss is the receiver's timing under the jammer). Ground reflection (V12): a specular ray 0.8 of the LoS at the measured 80 ns costs 0.6 dB (ideal) / 0.2 dB (real), so the flat-fading model holds; the receiver has no equalizer for a strong late echo, which the air-ground measurements do not show (late components more than 25 dB down, Sun).
- *Flight envelope.* Speed 0-161 km/h (rotorcraft measured while hovering, Khawaja et al.; Gomez-Ponce et al.; 161 km/h is the FAA small-UAV limit quoted by Khawaja et al.); heading turns per flight at a coordinated-turn rate up to the largest bank angle measured on a small UAV (57.9°, Gross et al.) and never above its largest measured yaw rate (28.7°/s, Allen & Lin); the directions of the GCS and of every interferer rotate across the array. Low edge-speed band 0-21 km/h.
- *Severity corrections (user approval):* spoofer up to 30 dB justified by geometry, a counterfeit GCS on the same radio as ours (Mekdad et al.) about 31 times closer to the UAV (capture from a 0.2-3 dB advantage, Whitehouse et al.); airframe shadowing as measured events, depth 6-25 dB (median 15.5 ± 4.9 dB at C-band, Sun et al.) with the hidden antenna's K-factor at -16 dB, instead of a constant 40 dB (a fading peak); receive correlation drawn per flight in 0.3-0.9 (the 0.3 had no source; on an aircraft underside the amplitudes, LoS included, correlate 0.85-0.99, Sun); antenna fault = a connector open for the flight (Fedde & Carter, US 4,506,385; opens are the most common connector failure, Ginart et al.), 26-36 dB stated as a gap-capacitance estimate (no measured depth exists; the 86-672 Hz vibration model is dropped: those are frame modes, Verbeke & Debruyne); WLAN idle gaps gamma-distributed with the field shape 0.49 (Cheema & Salous, where exponential fits worst), occupancy 5-86% (Wollenberg et al., lab cases; field 4.6-11.5%), level from the ETSI EN 300 328 limits and geometry, cap 30 dB.
- *Labels.* An antenna-fault frame in which the contact never opened was labelled a fault; frames are now labelled by what is on the air (as benign frames without a packet).
- *Hardware.* A gain and phase error per receive chain, fixed per flight (0.1 dB / 1°, Bakr); the adaptive combiner absorbs it. I/Q imbalance of an AD9361-class transceiver (0.2 % / 0.2°, image about 54 dB down) leaves even a 30 dB jammer's image far below our signal.
- *Unknown-threat score.* A new candidate with the input pre-processing of Lee et al. (step chosen on validation against FGSM versions); the leave-one-threat-out selection decides. If it is chosen, its extra gradient pass is measured in the latency stage (KPI 7, see D72).
- *Combined threats.* Four triples added (three emitters; two emitters with a lost antenna; a jammer in a banking turn at long range; a tone and a spoofer in a turn: Liu et al. "one or several jammers"; Yang et al.); every combination is also measured left out of training.
- *Overhead pass (stage OHP):* the dipole null below the UAV (-30 dB measured, Badi et al.) against the shorter distance: every antenna loses alike, up to about 23 dB below the cruise level at 300 m altitude, i.e. path loss within its trained levels.
- *Directional GCS antenna in every profile (stage GCSA, user decision; an earlier version of this entry had dropped it)*, under the rule that applies in Israel (Ministry of Communications exemption: 100 mW e.i.r.p. at 2.4 GHz, as ETSI EN 300 328, whose 10 mW in any 1 MHz caps our signal at 10.2 dBm, 95.5% of its power in its central 1 MHz (eirp_cap_dbm.m; corrected in D75); the stricter reading where unclear). The cap limits the radiated power, not the antenna: a directional antenna helps as far as the radio stays below the cap. The GCS is an AD9361-class radio (0.25 dB steps, up to 7.5 dBm for a CW tone at 2.4 GHz; corrected in D75) at -6 dBm, raised by its largest step under the cap as power_control, on a tracked 12 dBi antenna (the ground antenna of Rodriguez-Pineiro et al.'s air-ground measurements): 6 dBm e.i.r.p., 10 dB over an omni on the same radio, i.e. 10 dB less jammer-to-signal ratio for the same jammer and 3.2 times the range. The power step adds 4.0 dB, up to the cap (power_step_db.m; it was +6 dB on a profile A already at the cap, a breach of the cap, corrected). Pointing: the measured mean errors 5.62 deg azimuth / 1.51 deg elevation with a flying quadcopter (Nugroho & Dectaviansyah), half-normal, ITU-R F.1336 main lobe, drawn per flight and applied in the channel (gcs_pointing.m, block 'GCS'): 0.4 dB on average; no source gives the error versus speed or range, so no separate lag term. A lost target falls back to the omni (Boeing, US 8,503,941): 10 dB, inside the path-loss levels. No K-factor gain is claimed: a directional ground antenna lowered K in one measured environment and was mixed in the other (Rodriguez-Pineiro et al.). Eb/N0 includes the antenna gain; link-budget profile A is this GCS with its omni fallback.
- *Proposal review (2026-10-03, before any v7 reading):* the deployed policy is the DQN with escalation only when it passed its validation gate (recovery >= rule + escalation, false-alarm bound), otherwise the rule with escalation, as the proposal says; KPI 5 always compares the DQN itself with the rule. Latency (KPI 7) is measured on the idle machine after each version's runs, then KPI, DASH, WEAK and RPT again. Survivability options per profile (one antenna fewer, relay on the system's array).

## D75 — v7 gap fixes on the link side: delay spread, attitude, GCS direction, FEC packets, power step, sweeper timing
**Why (2026-10-04, with the user):** the ranked gap review of v7 before launch (link side: items 1, 3-6, 9, 10, 21, 24, 25) found single-tap channels where the sources measure 64-302 ns of delay spread, banked turns and wind without an RF effect, the GCS fixed at the array's broadside, an FEC codeword over a whole run, a power step no named radio has, and a sweeper timing without a source. The user's decisions (2026-10-04): implement the items; the hover wobble model; the space-time MMSE combiner if V12c fails; protocol-aware attacks are not implemented.
**Decision:**
- *Delay spread (item 1).* A tapped delay line on our signal and on every interferer: taps 250 ns apart up to 4.5 µs, an exponential profile matched to the RMS delay spread at the channel's K, the LoS on tap 0, every tap with its own sinusoids and its spatial signature centred on its source. V12c (validate_phy.m: real receiver, 64-1000 ns, clean link at K −5/2/10 dB under MRC, 16 and 30 dB noise jammers under MMSE at ρ 0.3 and 0.9, limits 0.5 and 3 dB) fails on the flat channel, so the line runs on every flight: RMS delay spreads drawn per flight, log-normal (Rodriguez-Pineiro et al., environment II: our signal on the directional OLoS fit (−6.52, 0.32) below 25 m and the omni fit (−6.63, 0.16) above; every interferer its own omni draw), clipped at 1 µs (their Fig. 7a), recorded in the dataset (ds_ns, ds_int) and in PP.cov. ## D76 — v7 detector: a fair reading beside KPI 1, WLAN gaps judged by their window, branch persistence, quiet-slot shape, partial antenna faults
**Why (2026-10-04, with the user, after the v6 detector diagnosis):** v6's fused test reading was accuracy 92.69%, macro-F1 92.21%, and 94.09% / 93.41% on the frames whose fusion window is full: sub-runs of 20 frames truncate the 12-cycle window (the first frame decides on itself). 419 of the 1427 clean test frames were WLAN frames without a packet inside a benign run (110 read as benign_interference, the same action); the antenna fault lost 28% of its frames to none and shadowing; benign interference read as none on a degraded link in 67 frames; the tone and the spoofer were confused in 12. The user approved seven items and wider antenna-fault levels, every one backed by a source (2026-10-04).
**Decision:**
- *D1, beside KPI 1 (KPI 1 unchanged: macro-F1 per Eb/N0 from the validation threshold, on the fused decisions).* eval_detector.m adds the steady-state reading (frames whose fusion window is full, rank ≥ N in their sub-run, with its bootstrap interval) and the onset latency per class on the test sub-runs (cycles to the first correct fused class and to the confirmed alarm: a hostile fused class on m of the last n cycles, decision_config.m; benign interference raises none). In the closed loop rollout_policy.m records per episode the cycles from the onset to the monitor's first class of the threat (t_cls) and to its first confirmed alarm (t_conf); evaluate_policies.m tabulates them per threat for the deployed policy (KP.onset). measure_all_kpis.m prints both beside KPI 1; export_report_numbers.m carries them (metrics.steady, metrics.onset, KP.onset).
- *D2, WLAN gaps.* The per-frame label stays as the proposal defines it (a frame without a packet is clean, mitigation 3). The fused decision is judged on its window (fusion_target.m): a clean frame whose window holds a benign_interference frame of its run has the target benign_interference, in the fusion's training and cross-validation (select_fusion.m), in the KPI 1 threshold on validation and in the fused readings (eval_detector.m; detector_block.m for eval_unseen_snr.m and eval_unseen_severity.m).
- *D3, branch imbalance over the window (temporal_evidence.m).* off_w, the mean per-cycle offset of the weakest antenna (lowest window mean) from the median of the others (Willgert, US 8,548,029, eq. 5, col. 11), and rat_w, its standard deviation over |off_w| (capped at 10): a loss in the RF path is |mean| > 3 dB with a ratio below 3, a pattern mismatch a mean ≤ 3 dB with a ratio above 3 (Table 1, col. 12); a sustained imbalance above 5 dB marks a degraded antenna connection (Heath, US 10,404,368, Table 2, cols. 41-42); the window mean is Lee's local average. A CUSUM over minutes (Xie et al.) cannot be validated in 0.6 s episodes and is not added: a field capability.
- *D4, the weakest antenna's K-factor.* k_w, the moment-method K = sqrt(Ga² − Gv²) / (Ga − sqrt(Ga² − Gv²)) of its per-cycle power (Aoki & Honda 2025, eq. 2, p. 12; in the library), clipped to −20..30 dB: inside airframe shadowing K falls from about 15 to −16 dB (Sun et al., Part IV, p. 5), a loss in series with the antenna keeps it. Read only over cycles in which every antenna's gain is above the noise of its 32-symbol channel estimate (est_margin > 0 dB, a new per-frame measurement: the lowest over the antennas of |h|² / (R_aa / 32) − 1), at least 3 of them; kv_w is the share of such cycles.
- *D5, the rule (rule_based_policy.m).* A degraded link whose class is none while the quiet slot shows interference (q_iot at or above q_thr) takes channel_switch (with spatial_diversity when the interferer is separable) instead of the generic diversity: low delivery at a high signal level is jamming (Xu et al. 2005, pp. 8-9); the slot's power is the AGC level of a jamming monitor (Ndili & Enge 1998, p. 5). q_thr comes from the clean validation frames as Kazim et al. set the AGC threshold from interference-free samples (eq. 1.3, p. 2, Th = μ − 3σ − 2 dB for a gain drop): μ + 3σ + 2 dB of q_iot (select_fusion.m, data/fusion.mat; default 3 dB, decision_config.m q_alarm_db); it reaches the rule through PP.q_thr (fusion_model.m: the pools, the check pools, the latency stage, the console). Benign interference on a degraded link already left the channel. The DQN sees the same evidence without a change to its training: q_iot of each of the last 4 cycles, occ_w, the degradation and the new window measurements are in its state.
- *D6, WLAN timing, from what the receiver has.* Per frame (extract_closed_loop_frames.m): duty, the share of the frame's 32-sample residual blocks on the reference antenna more than 3 dB over thermal (busy over observed time, Cheema & Salous 2019, eq. 1, p. 5; Airshark F3, p. 7), and edges, the busy/idle changes between them (burst length and envelope ripple, Grimaldi et al. 2019, p. 4; inter-pulse timing, Airshark, p. 5); over the window duty_w, their mean (the occupancy estimate beside occ_w of the quiet slot).
- *D7, the quiet slot's shape.* q_sfm, the spectral flatness exp(mean ln S) / mean S of the slot's periodogram averaged over the antennas, and q_par, the peak-to-average power of its envelope on the antenna with the most slot power: a tone is one spectral line and present in the slot (Shahriar et al. 2015, eq. 19, p. 7), a counterfeit of our modulation fills its band. Measured at 12 dB with 16 dB interferers (20 frames): tone 0.004-0.04 / 0.4-1.5 dB, spoofer 0.013-0.077 / 2.7-4.2 dB, barrage jammer about 0.5 / 7 dB, thermal noise 0.80-0.88 / 5-10 dB.
- *Antenna-fault levels.* From a partial loss to an open contact: decision levels 5 / 11.5 / 18 / 24.5 / 31 dB (decision_config.m), dataset levels eight even steps 5-31 dB (dataset_levels.m), the unseen-severity reading above them at 36 dB (an open contact of 0.01 pF). The backed range: a sustained diversity imbalance above 5 dB (Heath, Table 2), a steady RF-path loss above 3 dB (Willgert, Table 1); real connector faults are small losses or intermittent (Enquebecq et al., p. 6: fretting raises the loss from 0.15 to 0.37 dB; Smith et al. 2008, pp. 1-2). init_params.m: fault_atten_db 18 dB, the nominal level. The edge map marks every antenna-fault level as assumed.
- *EDGE.* DET stays the proposal's criterion; the commitment uses DET_h (checked: edge_commit.m and commit_rows read the harmed flights' verdict, D74). edge_map.m adds DET_u, the detection on the unharmed flights, beside it (per-point table, summary line, EDGE_NUM.det_split with the flight counts); no verdict rule changes.

## D77 — v7 receiver on the delay line: space-time sync, signal-free loaded covariance, frame-wide passes; receive front end
**Why (2026-10-04/05, with the user):** D75 left the receiver on the delay line failing V12c: a 30 dB jammer at every spread (its training-based sync broke: the frequency off by more than 500 Hz in 79-88% of frames, while known symbols at the true timing reach BER 4e-3) and a 16 dB jammer at ρ 0.3 from 234 ns. Diagnosis: 27 space-time dimensions estimated from 32-64 samples (SMI loss mean (K − N + 2)/(K + 1), Reed-Mallett-Brennan; Besson 2021, eq. 20-21) with our own signal inside the covariance (MPDR penalty, Besson eq. 22-23), and a first pass whose windows took their weights from the earlier windows' decisions. The user approved three source-backed fixes and, as a scope addition, a receive front end (AGC + ADC).
**Decision:**
- *Sync with space-time taps (R2).* Frequency first (Morelli & D'Amico, eq. 24 and 31; coarse then fine, Bertolucci et al., Fig. 3): at every arrival the training's 5-tap snapshots per antenna (±1 symbol, half a symbol apart), whitened by their own covariance loaded with the noise floor, are correlated with the training on the 512-point FFT grid (1.95 kHz bins over ±121 kHz), coherence a = g'(R + σ²I)^-1 g / K² (JASS, Marti, Arquint & Studer 2024, eq. 10 and Alg. 1: with the training projected out of R the statistic is a / (1 − a) by Sherman-Morrison, the same maximum; the loaded inverse in place of the projection, Besson 2021, eq. 81). The space-time taps sit on the arrival of the largest a; the arrival itself is the peak of the antennas' coherence (JASS's statistic) at that frequency within SPS + 2 samples: the space-time statistic is flat over about ±1 symbol and leans 3 samples early under a jammer, while the antennas' peak is within 0.5-1.2 samples of the true arrival in 90% of the dev frames. The residual frequency comes from the first pass, not from the pilot steps (under a jammer they leaned 150-400 Hz on the dev frames). The flat channel and the MRC on the delay line keep their sync (bit-identical with the front end off).
- *Noise floor.* The median eigenvalue of the quiet slot's 27-dimensional space-time covariance (9 taps per antenna): −1 to +4 dB from the thermal level under the 16-30 dB dispersive jammers of the dev frames; over 5 taps per antenna the jammer fills more than half of the dimensions and the estimate is up to 10 dB high (30 dB, 234 ns, 12 dB: BER 0.10 against 0.014).
- *Signal-free loaded covariance (R1).* Our signal's space-time channel by LS on the symbols around every snapshot (±4 symbols: the taps' ±2 and two raised-cosine side lobes); the interference + noise covariance from the residuals (the snapshots less our signal), never from the snapshots themselves; MVDR weights (R + ISI + μI)^-1 h₀, μ the noise floor (LSMI for non-stationary jammers: Gershman, Nickel & Böhme 1996, Sec. 3.2; Besson eq. 68 and 80).
- *First pass and passes (R3).* The first pass takes our channel from the training and, window by window (32 data symbols, rows within 64), the covariance of the residuals of the rows whose symbols are known or decided (with the training and the quiet slot while fewer than 54 rows): a decision error enters only a covariance row, never the weights' channel. Each window's phase from its pilots and decisions, the residual frequency from the slope of the window phases over the frame; twice. Then 4 passes over the whole frame: the channel by LS on all known and decided symbols (Marti, Kölle & Studer 2023, eq. 15; their regularized start, Sec. V-C, p. 7), the covariance of the residuals within 32 symbols of every 64-symbol window (the jammer's channel ages). The last pass leaves out the rows that carry a pilot and returns the pilots' error under its weights; the frame keeps that output or the antennas-alone one, whichever reproduces its pilots better (D75).
- *Sync-failure flag.* Receiver output 6, rows 6-7, in extract_closed_loop_frames F.sync_coh and F.sync_fail: the 5-tap space-time coherence of the training at the frame's arrival and frequency (every real-receiver configuration, 0..1) and the flag sync_coh < p.sync_coh_min = 0.60: the training was not found, the frame's symbols are erasures for a decoder. The threshold is the largest on a 0.05 grid that flags at most 1% of the correctly synchronized frames (frequency within 500 Hz of the genie run without the jammer) over dev flights (11 configurations, seeds 7001-7050, 4840 frames; never validate_phy's or the tests' flights): 0.95% of them flagged; it flags 27 of the 110 frames off by more than 500 Hz and 49 of the 140 lost frames (BER > 0.2). The other lost frames found their training (coherence near 0.7) and fail later in the frame.
- *Receive front end (scope addition, adc_frontend.m).* Per antenna an AGC and an ADC of p.adc_bits = 12 bits per rail, before synchronization and combining (0 or Inf: off). AD9361 data sheet Rev. F: 12-bit Σ-Δ ADCs (p. 33); Table 1: 1 dB gain step, 73 dB gain range at 2.3 GHz, RSSI ±2 dB; no AGC settling time and no receiver maximum input level. So the gain is held over the frame, set from the larger mean power of the quiet slot and the first 16 short-training symbols at the latest arrival (the short training serves AGC settling in 802.11), on the 1 dB grid; full scale p.adc_backoff_db = 12 dB (to 13 dB) above the rail RMS: the 1 − 10⁻⁴ quantile of the composite's rails on the dev frames is 11.1-12.1 dB (a Gaussian's 11.8 dB), so 2-9·10⁻⁵ of the rails clip; SQNR 63-64 dB. Resolution under jamming: one bit per 6.02 dB of jammer (Marti et al. 2024, Prop. 2, eq. 18-19), 8 bits for a 25 dB jammer (Castaneda et al. 2021), up to 6 dB of anti-jam per A/D bit (Knight, Cahn & Nair 2007): with 12 bits under a 30 dB jammer the quantization noise stays about 23 dB below the thermal floor.
- *Absolute level at the UAV.* The 802.11 receiver maximum input level at 2.4 GHz is −20 dBm (Yucek & Kim 2011, 802.11-11/1077r0; Hart 2022, 802.11-21/1563r3); the AD9361 data sheet gives IIP3 −14 dBm at maximum gain and no maximum input. At the closest committed point (18 dB, 0.20 km, power step on: 10 dBm e.i.r.p.) our signal arrives at −74 dBm in free space and the 30 dB-capped jammer at −44 dBm, 24 dB below −20 dBm. Over the flight geometry our signal is largest at −49 dBm (15 m altitude, 5.8 m from the mast, dipole −3.3 dB); with the cap a jammer could reach −19 dBm there, 1 dB above the 802.11 level, about 57 dB of Eb/N0, far outside the simulated grid: front-end compression is not modelled and that close pass is a stated limit.
- *Pools.* MODEL_TAG 'v7-D77' (build_policy_pools.m, with D76's detector measurements): pools built on the D75 receiver are not reused.
