### Takeaway
The three algorithmic foundations (Mnih 2015, van Hasselt 2016, Alshiekh 2018) are primary, peer-reviewed and fully read; they back the mechanisms the project uses (replay, target network, Double-DQN target, preemptive shield, hold rule) but none of the project's numeric hyperparameters is copied from them. #### P08 - Mnih et al., "Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning", Nature 518:529-533 (2015), doi:10.1038/nature14236 (local L/P08_Mnih2015_DQN_Nature.pdf; extracts X/P08_Mnih2015_DQN_Nature.txt and E/P08L.txt)
- Method: deep Q-network on 49 Atari games, same architecture and hyperparameters for all games; one output unit per action so all Q-values come from one forward pass (Methods, "Model architecture", X/P08:380) — [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)
- Instability of Q-learning with a nonlinear approximator is attributed to three causes: correlated observation sequences, policy changes shifting the data distribution, and correlation between Q and its targets (main text, X/P08:24-34) — [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)
- Fixes: experience replay with uniform sampling from the last N transitions, and a target network cloned every C updates (Methods, X/P08:536-612); error term of the update clipped to [-1, 1], which the authors state is equivalent to an absolute-value loss outside (-1, 1) (i.e. Huber with threshold 1; the word "Huber" is not used) (X/P08:616-637) — [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)
- Partial observability: since one screen does not reveal the full state, the input is a sequence of observations and actions; in practice the preprocessing stacks the m = 4 most recent frames, and the authors say the algorithm is robust to other m such as 3 or 5 (Methods, X/P08:380, X/P08:390) — [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)
- Training values stated in text: RMSProp, minibatch 32; epsilon-greedy annealed linearly 1.0 -> 0.1 over the first million frames then fixed at 0.1; 50 million training frames; replay memory of the 1 million most recent frames; frame skip k = 4; discount 0.99; rewards clipped to +-1 during training; evaluation 30 games of up to 5 min with epsilon = 0.05 (X/P08:381-391) — [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)
- Hyperparameters were set by an informal search on five games, without a systematic grid search (Methods, X/P08:384; Extended Data Table 1 caption) — [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)
- Results: beats the best prior RL methods on 43 of 49 games; above 75% of the human tester's score on 29 games (X/P08:244) — [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)
- Ablation (Extended Data Table 3): replay and target network each matter; disabling them is detrimental (X/P08:313). The numeric table and Extended Data Table 1 (target-update period C, etc.) are images not present in either extract: UNVERIFIED from text. van Hasselt's appendix states its DQN runs use Mnih's exact conditions and lists target-update period 10,000 (X/P09_vanHasselt2016_Double_DQN_AAAI.txt:341, 864) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)
- Future-work remark: uniform replay treats all transitions equally; a sampling strategy emphasising transitions with most to learn, akin to prioritized sweeping, is suggested (X/P08:586-594, 332) — [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)

#### P09 - van Hasselt, Guez, Silver, "Deep Reinforcement Learning with Double Q-learning", AAAI-16 (extract is arXiv:1509.06461v3) (X/P09_vanHasselt2016_Double_DQN_AAAI.txt, X/P09_compact.txt, X/P09_layout.txt)
- Double DQN target: the online network selects the argmax action at the next state, the target network evaluates it; target network update unchanged from DQN (periodic copy); described as the minimal change from DQN (X/P09:283-330) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)
- Theorem 1: if all true action values are equal and the estimates are unbiased on average but not all correct (mean squared error C > 0, m >= 2 actions), then max_a Q_t(s,a) >= V*(s) + sqrt(C/(m-1)); the bound is tight; Double Q-learning's lower bound on absolute error is zero (X/P09:101-134; the square root is lost in the extract and restored here from the formula structure) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)
- Dependence on the number of actions: the Theorem-1 lower bound decreases with m (an artefact of a lower bound); the text then states, quoting: "More typically, the overoptimism increases with the number of actions" (X/P09:136, shown in Figure 1, a synthetic example). Thrun & Schwartz's earlier upper bound for errors uniform in [-eps, eps] scales with gamma (the fraction is garbled in the extract; X/P09:97) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)
- Empirics: DQN overestimated values in all 49 tested games; Double DQN more stable (Fig. 3, 6 seeds, X/P09:459, 476) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)
- Scores (normalized to human): no-op starts, median 93.5% (DQN) vs 114.7% (Double DQN), mean 241.1% vs 330.3%; human starts, median 47.5% / 88.4% / 116.7% (DQN / Double DQN / Double DQN tuned), mean 122.0% / 273.1% / 475.2% (X/P09_compact.txt:314-347) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)
- Tuned version: target-copy interval raised from 10,000 to 30,000 frames to reduce overestimation further, training epsilon 0.1 -> 0.01, evaluation epsilon 0.001, one shared bias for all action values (X/P09:508, 537) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)
- Setup (appendix): gamma 0.99, learning rate 0.00025, target update every 10,000 steps, 50M steps, evaluation every 1M steps with the best policy across evaluations kept as output, replay 1M, update every 4 steps, minibatch 32, epsilon 1 -> 0.1 over 1M steps, RMSProp momentum 0.95 (X/P09:862-864) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)

#### P11 - Alshiekh, Bloem, Ehlers, Koenighofer, Niekum, Topcu, "Safe Reinforcement Learning via Shielding", AAAI-18 (library PDF and extract are arXiv:1708.08611v2, the extended version; section numbers below are of that version) (X/P11_Alshiekh2018_Safe_RL_Shielding_AAAI.txt)
- A shield is a reactive system synthesized from a temporal-logic safety specification plus a finite abstraction of the environment; it guarantees safety and minimum interference, i.e. it forbids an action only if it could endanger safe behaviour (Sec. 1, X/P11:79) — [Alshiekh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- Two placements: preemptive (before the agent; outputs the set of safe actions each step) and post-posed (after the agent; replaces an unsafe chosen action) (Sec. 1 and 5.1-5.2, X/P11:81, 353-357, 476) — [Alshiekh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- Preemptive shielding is equivalent to a new MDP whose state is the product of MDP state and shield state, with the unsafe actions removed (Sec. 5.1 "Properties", X/P11:474) — [Alshiekh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- Assumption 1: environment is an MDP, an abstraction exists, and the learner accepts (MDP state x shield state) as input (X/P11:348); convergence (Sec. 7): any learner that converges on standard MDPs converges with a shield, via the product MDP, using the fact that the learner observes the shield state; for single-state specifications the learner may observe only the MDP state (X/P11:582-587) — [Alshiekh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- Post-posed variants: either punish the unsafe action (then the shield must stay on after learning) or give it the substituted action's reward; the agent may supply a ranking of actions and the shield executes the first allowed one (Sec. 5.2 and 6, X/P11:477-482, 572) — [Alshiekh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- Water-tank example: valve setting must be kept at least three seconds after any switch, tank must never overflow or run dry; formalized as an LTL safety formula; product game 602 states; the Sec. 8.4 version states the inflow switch may not change mode before 3 time steps since the last change; post-posed shield synthesized in under a second (Sec. 4 Example 1, X/P11:290-293, 500, 714) — [Alshiekh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- Experiments: grid worlds with tabular Q-learning (shielded agents never get negative reward and tend to learn faster), self-driving car (shielded learns faster, never crashes), Atari Seaquest with an off-the-shelf DQN used as a black box (shield did not change performance; no safety violations), water tank with Q-learning and SARSA (shielded reach the optimum in significantly shorter time) (Sec. 8, X/P11:589-718) — [Alshiekh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)

#### W02 - Schaul, Quan, Antonoglou, Silver, "Prioritized Experience Replay", ICLR 2016 (arXiv:1511.05952v4) (X/W02_Schaul2016_Prioritized_Experience_Replay.txt)
- Method: replay transitions with probability proportional to p_i^alpha, p_i = |TD error| + eps (proportional) or 1/rank (rank-based); stochastic prioritization keeps diversity; importance-sampling weights (normalized by their max) correct the bias, with beta annealed linearly to 1 by the end of training (Sec. 3.3-3.4, X/W02:39-92) — [Schaul 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)
- Results: DQN + PER better on 41 of 49 games, median normalized score 48% -> 106%, mean 122% -> 355%; on top of tuned Double DQN median 111% -> 128% (proportional; rank-based 113%), mean 418% -> 551% (rank-based 454%); learning about twice as fast (Table 1, X/W02:147-178, 193) — [Schaul 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)
- Chosen hyperparameters (Table 3): rank-based alpha 0.7, beta 0.5 -> 1; proportional alpha 0.6, beta 0.4 -> 1; step size reduced to one quarter of 0.00025 (X/W02:279-297) — [Schaul 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)
- Caveat stated by the authors: |TD error| ignores inherent stochasticity of rewards/transitions and limits from partial observability or approximator capacity, so it is problematic when some transitions are unlearnable; rank-based was expected to be more robust to outliers (Appendix A, X/W02:213; Sec. 5, X/W02:182) — [Schaul 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)
- Related extension: prioritized sampling on a severely class-imbalanced MNIST approaches imbalance-informed performance (Sec. 6, Fig. 5, X/W02:186-190) — [Schaul 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)

#### W01 - Sagduyu, Erpek, Davaslioglu, Kompella (Nexcepta), "How to Combat Reactive and Dynamic Jamming Attacks with Reinforcement Learning", arXiv:2510.02265v1 (Oct 2025), preprint (X/W01_2025_Reactive_Dynamic_Jamming_RL.txt)
- Jammer: energy detector with sensing threshold that switches between tau_low (after a slot without jamming) and tau_high (after jamming); jams with the detection probability; in the multichannel case the jammer stays on its channel with probability p = 0.8 after a successful jam and with q = 0.2 otherwise, and lowers its threshold when moving (Sec. - Critique of the literature: most RL anti-jamming studies treat each slot as an independent decision with an exogenous jammer, which collapses to a bandit and yields policies that fail when the jammer adapts to the transmitter; here the jammer's next state depends on the transmitter's action (Sec. I, X/W01:9-11). This is an argument; the paper runs no gamma = 0 ablation (only gamma = 0.95, Table I) — [Sagduyu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02265)
- Learned behaviour: RL lowers transmit power so the signal at the jammer stays under its (unknown) sensing threshold, cutting the jamming rate; fixed max power is ineffective (Sec. III, Figs. 1-3) — [Sagduyu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02265)
- Parameters (Table I): 101 power levels, tau_low 0.2, tau_high 0.4, 20,000 episodes x 200 steps, alpha 0.1, gamma 0.95, epsilon 1.00 -> 0.01, decay 0.999, h_TJ changing every 5,000 episodes; DQN for the continuous state: two hidden layers of 64, replay 100,000, minibatch 64 (X/W01:95, 481) — [Sagduyu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02265)
- Total reward: power control RL 36,552.44 vs fixed 428.54; power+modulation 37,053.69 vs 12,059.80; two channels 43,736.50 vs 22,195.21; DQN with continuous received-power state lower than tabular (35,972.87 single, 39,439.82 multi) (Tables II-V) — [Sagduyu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02265)
- Environment: free-space toy geometry, two jammer positions, N = 2 channels, Shannon/uncoded-QAM throughput reward (Sec. II) — [Sagduyu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02265)

## Q3. Unused content that could strengthen the decision layer (adaptive adversary, unknown threats, partial observability, safe sample efficiency)

### Takeaway
The sources hold concrete, citable jammer models for the planned v7 adaptive adversary (W01 threshold-adapting reactive jammer, Yuan's win-stay/lose-shift comb jammer, Grekhov's transition-learning predictive jammer, S14's history-blocking jammer, Amuru's learning jammer) and the matching defences (concealment by low power, unpredictable channel choice, deception). For unknown threats and partial observability they offer robust training (W03), recurrent or longer histories tied to the jammer's memory (Yuan, S14, Hausknecht), and continual learning (Davaslioglu). For safe sample efficiency: rank-based prioritized replay, auxiliary prediction tasks, and rule-guided exploration.

### Cited Findings
Adaptive adversary
- A reactive jammer whose energy-detection threshold rises after it jams and falls after it misses, and which stays on a channel with probability 0.8 after a hit and 0.2 otherwise, is a fully parameterized, sourced model of an adaptive jammer whose behaviour depends on the defender's actions (Sec. II, V, Table I) — [Sagduyu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02265)
- Against such a jammer the learned defence is to lower transmit power below the jammer's sensing threshold (concealment); the project's action set has power +6 dB but no power reduction (Sec. III) — [Sagduyu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02265); [Yang 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11687)
- Yuan's intelligent frequency pattern switches between two comb patterns whenever the user was not jammed in the previous slot (Sec. 6.2.2, X/F05:1447): a simple, sourced adaptive rule for a v7 jammer — [Yuan 2021](https://doi.org/10.1049/cmu2.12257)
- A jammer that learns the defender's hop-transition statistics (first-order Markov counts with a Dirichlet prior) is neutralized only by an unpredictable (near-uniform) channel choice; an entropy bonus produced that policy (Sec. 2-4) — [Grekhov manuscript](L/N02_Grekhov2026_DQN_Resilient_Drone_Markov_Jammers.pdf); Oli & Mahalal also point to QRNG-driven hop unpredictability (E/P01r:235) — [Oli & Mahalal 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3647023)
- S14's intelligent blocking jammer (every 5 slots, the channels used in the past 5 slots plus 4 neighbours) is another sourced memory-based jammer (X/S14:181) — [Zhang 2025 SciRep](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-90201-1)
- W03's jammer 1 detects power above a threshold in one slot and attacks the next (Sec. V) — [Zhao 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03305)
- Deceit: deliberately suboptimal transmitter behaviour to mislead an intelligent jammer's predictor, or decoys that attract jamming (E/N01r:69) — [Yang 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11687); RL-based deception of reactive jammers into a victim channel reaches over 50% of the jam-free received power for one user and three channels (abstract read only; body UNVERIFIED) — [Pourranjbar 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14056)
- A learning (multi-armed bandit) jammer that tunes signaling scheme, power and on-off/pulse duration, analysed against static and adaptive victim pairs, with sub-linear convergence (abstract and introduction read; theorems UNVERIFIED) — [Amuru 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.3652)
- With action-dependent jammers the problem is a genuine MDP, and one-step (bandit) training can fail against adaptation (argument, no ablation) (Sec. I) — [Sagduyu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02265)
- Papathanasiou explicitly asks for resilience against adaptive and multi-vector threats, supporting triple threats and an adaptive adversary in v7 (Sec. 4-5) — [Papathanasiou 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/app16073339)

Unknown threats, partial observability, robustness
- PGD-DDQN trains on worst-case bounded observation perturbations; NQC-DDQN compresses Q intervals; decision accuracy under perturbation 42.2% (plain) vs about 83.3% (NQC), at a 2-3% nominal cost (Sec. III-V) — [Zhao 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03305)
- A temporal index in the state separates contexts that look alike but need different actions against reactive jammers (X/W03:124) — [Zhao 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03305)
- History should cover the jammer's memory: Yuan's Markov argument (X/F05:643) and S14's seq_len study (X/S14:201); both use sense-action pairs (observations and past actions), as does Mnih's definition of the state sequence (X/P08:390) — [Yuan 2021](https://doi.org/10.1049/cmu2.12257); [Zhang 2025 SciRep](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-90201-1); [Mnih 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236)
- DRQN matches frame-stacked DQN for the same history length, and degrades less than DQN when trained on full observations and evaluated on partial ones, i.e. it adapts better when observation quality changes at evaluation (abstract, E/HS.txt) — [Hausknecht & Stone 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06527)
- Catastrophic forgetting: a DRL anti-jamming agent forgets earlier jammer patterns while adapting to new ones; PackNet-style parameter isolation retains them (abstract and conclusion read; numbers UNVERIFIED) — relevant to adding a newly identified threat to the decision layer — [Davaslioglu 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10521)
- Open challenges named by Yang: decisions with partial information, robustness/generalization to unknown jamming strategies; meta-learning cited for rapid adaptation to dynamic jamming (Sec. VI-A, V) — [Yang 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11687)
- Safety layer at action execution (Control Barrier Functions) as a complement to learned policies (E/N01r:188) — [Yang 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11687)

Safe sample efficiency
- Prioritized replay: about 2x faster learning and higher scores on top of Double DQN; rank-based expected more robust to outliers; TD-error priority is unreliable for stochastic or partially observed transitions, which the project's fading link is (Sec. 5, App. A) — [Schaul 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)
- Prioritized sampling on class-imbalanced data approached imbalance-aware performance, which supports oversampling rare (threat x jammer-mode) groups without TD priorities (Sec. 6) — [Schaul 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)
- Auxiliary prediction task trained jointly with the DQN cut training episodes by up to 70% against a DRL jammer (Sec. 4-5) — [Zhang 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04963)
- Conventional-solution-aided acceleration and policy reuse/distillation: a known rule guides or competes with the agent early in training (Sec. III-IV) — [Nagib 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13532); Zhao et al. 2025 (Commun. Lett., "under rule guidance", cited as W03 ref. [21]) is the anti-jamming instance (not read; UNVERIFIED) — [Zhao 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03305)
- Shielded learners reached the optimum faster in the grid-world and water-tank experiments, and a shield on a black-box DQN did not change Seaquest performance (Sec. 8) — [Alshiekh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- Automatic epsilon-decay adjustment reached near-optimal performance from a large initial decay factor (Abstract, Sec. on ERDFAA) — [Zhang 2025 SciRep](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-90201-1)
- Longer target-copy period and lower final epsilon improved Double DQN (tuned version) (X/P09:508, 537) — [van Hasselt 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461)
